# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's simple AI lesson is:

**Use AI to draft customer replies, then make the final message yours.**

Recent small-business guidance highlights two things at once: AI can help with routine communication, and a person should review the result before it reaches a customer. The goal is a useful draft, not a fully automatic promise to someone you serve.

## The 20-Minute Exercise

1. Choose five old, routine customer questions. Remove names, contact details, and private information.
2. Write down your approved facts: services, hours, booking link, and policies.
3. Ask AI to draft one short reply to each question using only those facts.
4. Check every draft for accuracy, tone, and promises.
5. Save the two best replies as templates for future use.

Try questions about hours, availability, service areas, preparation steps, or how to book. Leave disputes, refunds, medical or legal questions, and unusual complaints to a qualified person.

## Copy This Prompt

```text
Draft a friendly customer reply for my business.

Customer question: [paste a de-identified question]
Approved business facts: [paste only current, public or approved facts]
Our tone: [three words, such as warm, direct, professional]

Requirements:
- Keep it under 120 words.
- Answer only from the approved facts.
- Do not invent availability, prices, policies, or guarantees.
- If a needed fact is missing, write [OWNER TO CONFIRM].
- End with one clear next step.
- Produce a draft only. Do not send anything.
```

## The Human Check

Before sending, confirm:

- Is the answer correct and current?
- Does it address the actual question?
- Does it sound like this business?
- Are links, dates, prices, and policies accurate?
- Would a reasonable customer understand the next step?

If the answer fails any check, revise it. If it requires a judgment call, write the reply yourself.

## A Beginner Service You Can Learn to Offer

**Customer Reply Starter Kit**

Deliverables: a short voice guide, ten approved reply templates, a reusable prompt, and a human-review checklist.

Learning curve: easy. Start with your own practice examples before offering it to a business. The work becomes medium difficulty when you must collect accurate policies, handle exceptions, and train a team to use the templates consistently.

Measure value honestly: time to draft, time to review, corrections needed, and whether customers still receive a personal answer. Never promise that AI will replace customer service or guarantee revenue.

## A1 Takeaway

AI can remove the blank page. The business still owns the facts, the tone, and the final send.
