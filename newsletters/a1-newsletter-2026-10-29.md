Subject: Build an AI-assisted FAQ that customers can trust

Preview text: A 30-minute workflow for turning approved business information into clear, source-backed answers without letting AI invent policy.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Customers repeatedly ask about hours, appointments, delivery, returns, pricing, accessibility, and what happens next. A useful FAQ can reduce repetitive messages, but a polished wrong answer creates more work than no answer at all.

AI can organize approved information and draft plain-language responses. The valuable skill is making every answer traceable to a current source and a responsible owner.

## Today's lesson: Build the source table first

Before drafting the FAQ, create a table with these columns:

- **Question:** The wording customers actually use.
- **Approved answer:** The current policy or process.
- **Source:** The document, page, or person that confirms it.
- **Owner:** Who can approve or update the answer?
- **Last reviewed:** When was the information checked?
- **Escalation:** Where should the customer go if the FAQ does not solve the problem?

AI should draft from this table, not from memory or a vague request to "write our policies."

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional business or five approved, low-risk questions from a real organization.

1. Collect only current, approved source material.
2. Remove private, confidential, customer-specific, and unnecessary information.
3. Write each customer question in plain language.
4. Ask AI to draft one short answer using only the supplied source.
5. Require the answer to name its source ID and mark missing information as **Needs owner review**.
6. Ask a person who owns the process to verify each answer.
7. Test the FAQ with alternate wording, misspellings, and follow-up questions.
8. Publish contact and escalation options for situations the FAQ cannot handle.
9. Add a review date and repeat the check whenever the underlying policy changes.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious FAQ drafting assistant. Use only the approved source table below.
Do not invent policy, prices, eligibility, deadlines, guarantees, exceptions, contact
details, or legal terms.

For each customer question, return:
1. A direct answer in plain language
2. The source ID used
3. The information owner
4. The last-reviewed date
5. The escalation route
6. A confidence label: Source-supported, Needs owner review, or No approved answer

Keep answers short and preserve every condition and exception. If sources conflict,
do not choose between them. Quote the conflict briefly and label it for owner review.
Suggest three alternate question phrasings customers may use, but do not change the answer.

Approved source table:
[Paste de-identified, approved information with stable source IDs]
```

## Human review checklist

- Every public answer has a current approved source.
- A named owner verified the policy or process.
- Prices, hours, dates, deadlines, locations, links, and contact details were tested.
- Conditions and exceptions were not removed for brevity.
- Conflicting or missing information was escalated instead of guessed.
- The writing is clear to someone unfamiliar with the organization.
- The page works on a phone and with keyboard navigation.
- Customers can reach a person or official channel when needed.
- A review date and update process are documented.
- High-stakes questions are routed to qualified staff rather than answered automatically.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Source-Backed FAQ Setup**. Interview the business owner, collect approved public information, build the source table, draft 10 to 20 answers, create an owner-review sheet, and publish the approved FAQ on the client's existing website or a simple no-code page.

Do not sell an unsupervised "AI customer service bot" before the business has clean source material and an escalation process. Start with a static FAQ that can be reviewed line by line. It is easier to test, update, and explain.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Draft and verify 10 source-backed FAQ answers for one simple service.
- **Medium:** Add categories, search terms, analytics, multilingual review, accessibility checks, and scheduled owner approvals.
- **Hard:** Build a governed knowledge system with permissions, retrieval, citations, versioning, automated tests, escalation, audit logs, privacy controls, and monitored customer-support integrations.

## What's new

Google announced on October 19, 2026 that interactive apps built in the Gemini app can be exported to a new private Google Sites draft for editing and publication. That can lower the technical barrier for lightweight tools, but publication still requires content review, access decisions, testing, and an update owner. A generated interface does not make its answers correct.

## Free-first learning path

1. Build the source table in a free spreadsheet using fictional business information.
2. Draft and verify 10 answers with a free AI tool and no sensitive data.
3. Publish a basic FAQ in free Google Sites or the client's existing site.
4. Ask five people to find an answer and record where they struggle.
5. Improve the source table and page before paying for chatbot or automation software.

## The takeaway

The dependable product is not an AI answer. It is a maintained chain from customer question to approved source, responsible owner, review date, and human help.
