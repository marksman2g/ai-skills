# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's practical lesson is: **use AI to draft an FAQ from questions customers actually ask.**

Current FAQ guidance agrees on two useful habits: write questions in the customer's language and answer with facts the business has approved. A generated list of imaginary questions is less valuable than ten real questions from calls, emails, estimates, and support conversations.

## The 30-Minute Exercise

1. Collect ten repeated questions from public comments or owner-approved, de-identified messages.
2. Group duplicate questions together.
3. Create a fact sheet with current hours, services, service area, process, policies, and official links.
4. Let AI draft short answers using only that fact sheet.
5. Have the business owner verify every answer before publishing.

## Copy This Prompt

```text
Draft a customer FAQ using only the approved questions and facts below.

For each item:
1. Keep the customer's natural wording.
2. Answer directly in 40 to 70 words.
3. Add one useful official link when provided.
4. Mark missing information as [OWNER TO CONFIRM].

Do not invent prices, availability, policies, guarantees, credentials,
service areas, turnaround times, or customer claims.

Questions:
[paste de-identified questions]

Approved facts and links:
[paste current business facts]
```

## Quality Check

- Is this a question customers really ask?
- Is the answer complete without marketing filler?
- Does every detail match the approved fact sheet?
- Is there a clear next step or official link?
- Does the question need its own full webpage instead?

Add an owner and review date to the FAQ. Business details change, so an accurate FAQ needs maintenance.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer a **Ten-Question FAQ Starter Pack**.

Deliverables: question inventory, approved fact sheet, ten reviewed answers, suggested page placement, source links, and a quarterly review checklist.

Learning curve: easy to medium. A static FAQ is often the right first step. A chatbot adds integrations, monitoring, privacy, failure handling, and ongoing support, so do not sell one before the source information is accurate.

## Skill Ladder

- Easy: collect questions and draft checked answers.
- Medium: organize a searchable knowledge base and track unanswered questions.
- Hard: connect a governed support assistant to current systems with permissions, escalation, monitoring, and human handoff.

## Takeaway

Start with real questions and approved facts. Good source information matters more than a flashy chatbot.
