Subject: Turn an approved proposal into a safer project kickoff plan with AI

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for creating a kickoff brief, responsibility table, risk register, and first-week plan without changing the deal.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

A signed proposal may describe what a client bought, but a team still needs a practical starting point: who owns each result, what happens first, which information is missing, and what could delay the work.

AI can organize those details into a strong first draft. The danger is that it can also add sensible-sounding tasks, dates, or responsibilities that nobody approved. The useful skill is creating a **source-backed kickoff pack that preserves the agreed scope and keeps every new suggestion visible for review**.

## Today's lesson: Separate commitments from planning suggestions

Build the kickoff pack with two layers:

- **Commitment layer:** Scope, deliverables, exclusions, dates, prices, acceptance criteria, and client responsibilities copied from the approved agreement.
- **Planning layer:** Suggested tasks, owners, dependencies, risks, questions, and working dates created to help the team execute.

Label every planning item as one of these:

- **AGREED:** Directly supported by the approved proposal or contract.
- **CONFIRMED AT KICKOFF:** Explicitly accepted by an authorized participant.
- **PROPOSED:** A useful suggestion that still needs approval.
- **OPEN QUESTION:** Missing information that could affect the plan.
- **OUT OF SCOPE:** Work that is not included in the agreement.

This keeps a helpful AI suggestion from quietly becoming a new client promise.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional project or documents you are authorized to process. Do not upload contracts, personal information, credentials, confidential pricing, or regulated data to an unapproved AI service.

1. Copy the exact project objective, deliverables, exclusions, acceptance criteria, and agreed dates into a source table.
2. Add the document name, section, page, or approved note that supports each item.
3. Ask AI to draft a one-page kickoff brief using only those confirmed facts.
4. Create a responsibility table with one accountable owner for each deliverable and a separate list of contributors and reviewers.
5. Generate a first-week task list, but label every new task, owner, and date `PROPOSED`.
6. Build a small risk register with risk, evidence, likelihood, impact, prevention, response owner, and next review date.
7. List dependencies, access needs, client inputs, and unanswered questions separately.
8. Compare every draft item with the approved scope and mark extra work `OUT OF SCOPE`.
9. Review the pack with the project owner, delivery team, and client decision-maker.
10. Record what was approved, update the version, and move only confirmed tasks into the team's project tool.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious project-kickoff assistant. Use only the approved proposal,
agreement, and kickoff notes below. Do not change the commercial agreement.

First create a source table with:
- objective
- deliverable
- exclusion
- acceptance criterion
- agreed date or milestone
- client responsibility
- dependency
- source document and exact section

Then create a DRAFT kickoff pack containing:
1. one-page project brief
2. deliverables and acceptance criteria
3. responsibility table with one accountable owner per deliverable
4. first-week task list
5. dependency and access checklist
6. risk register
7. decisions made
8. open questions
9. out-of-scope requests
10. approvals needed before work starts

Label every item AGREED, CONFIRMED AT KICKOFF, PROPOSED, OPEN QUESTION,
or OUT OF SCOPE.

Rules:
- do not invent or modify scope, pricing, deadlines, staffing, or legal terms
- do not assign a person unless the source confirms that assignment
- do not turn a suggested date into a commitment
- do not hide contradictions between documents
- preserve exact names, numbers, dates, and acceptance language when confirmed
- write [OWNER NEEDED], [DATE NEEDED], or [CLIENT CONFIRMATION NEEDED]
  when information is missing
- cite the source for every agreed commitment
- present the output as a draft for human approval

Approved source material:
[Insert authorized documents and notes]
```

## Human review checklist

- The proposal, agreement, and notes are the latest approved versions.
- The AI tool is authorized for the information being processed.
- Every agreed commitment has an exact source reference.
- Suggested tasks and dates remain visibly different from contractual commitments.
- Every deliverable has one accountable owner and clear reviewers.
- Client responsibilities, access needs, dependencies, and acceptance criteria are explicit.
- Risks are tied to evidence instead of invented probabilities.
- Scope conflicts and out-of-scope requests are visible.
- Legal, privacy, security, procurement, accessibility, financial, and compliance questions went to qualified owners.
- Only approved tasks were transferred into the live project system.
- The kickoff pack has a version, owner, approval record, and next review date.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI-Assisted Project Kickoff Pack** for one small, low-risk project. Deliver a source table, one-page brief, responsibility matrix, first-week plan, dependency checklist, risk register, decision log, open-question list, and kickoff agenda.

The client supplies the approved agreement and authorized decision-makers. You organize the material and prepare a reviewable draft; you do not change scope, assign authority, approve legal terms, or promise dates on anyone's behalf.

## What's current

Microsoft's October 2026 Planner update says AI-generated plans, tasks, and changes are presented in draft mode so users can review, edit, and approve them before they are applied. Current Planner guidance also says Copilot can generate plans from Word, Excel, or PowerPoint files and help create or update tasks through natural-language requests.

Microsoft's project guidance describes AI-generated task plans, risk assessments, and status reports as assistive recommendations that project managers can edit, remove, or expand. This supports a sensible operating rule: use AI to prepare the plan, but keep scope interpretation, ownership, dates, and acceptance with authorized people.

PMI's October 2026 project-management guidance gives practical examples for using AI to prepare meetings, compare transcripts with agendas, capture decisions and action items, review risks, and improve stakeholder communication. The repeated theme is support for alignment and follow-through, not transferring project accountability to the tool.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional proposal in a free document or spreadsheet tool.
2. Build the commitment table manually and require a source for every row.
3. Create the first-week plan with sticky notes or a free task board.
4. Ask a second person to find unsupported owners, dates, or scope changes.
5. Complete PMI's free five-hour practical generative-AI course or a free project-management or workflow badge before buying software.
6. Add paid AI planning tools only after the review, approval, and change-control steps work without them.

## The takeaway

AI can shorten the distance between a signed proposal and an organized kickoff. The professional value comes from preserving the agreement, showing what is merely proposed, assigning real owners, and approving the plan before execution begins.
