# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Writing a client proposal can take hours because the writer must turn a messy conversation into a clear offer. AI can help organize the information and prepare a first draft, but it cannot know what the business can truly deliver, how long the work will take, or what price is profitable unless qualified people provide those decisions.

The beginner-friendly skill is not asking AI to "write a winning proposal." It is building a decision sheet that separates confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions.

## Today's lesson: Decide first, draft second

Create a proposal decision sheet with these sections:

- **Client goal:** The result the client described in their own words.
- **Current problem:** What is happening now and what evidence supports it?
- **Success measure:** How the client and provider will know the work helped.
- **Included work:** Specific deliverables and responsibilities.
- **Excluded work:** Tasks, systems, revisions, or outcomes not included.
- **Dependencies:** Information, access, approvals, people, and decisions the client must provide.
- **Timeline inputs:** Confirmed dates, review periods, and schedule risks.
- **Pricing inputs:** Approved rates, quantities, expenses, taxes, and payment rules.
- **Open questions:** Anything that could change scope, price, risk, or timing.
- **Approvers:** Who validates scope, pricing, legal terms, and the final send.

AI may organize those inputs. Humans must make the commitments.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional project or approved, de-identified client notes.

1. Remove personal, confidential, and commercially sensitive details that the AI tool is not approved to receive.
2. Extract every stated goal, deliverable, date, constraint, and question from the notes.
3. Label each item **Confirmed**, **Assumption**, **Open**, or **Out of scope**.
4. Ask AI to create the decision sheet without resolving unknowns.
5. Have the delivery owner confirm capacity, responsibilities, and exclusions.
6. Have the pricing owner calculate and approve every amount outside the AI draft.
7. Ask AI to draft the proposal using only confirmed decisions.
8. Compare the draft line by line with the decision sheet.
9. Run a contradiction check across scope, timeline, price, and terms.
10. Require final human approval before the proposal is sent.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious proposal-drafting assistant. Use only the approved decision
sheet below. Do not invent capabilities, deliverables, staffing, credentials,
case studies, customer results, deadlines, prices, discounts, legal terms, or
acceptance criteria.

First return a readiness table with these columns:
- decision
- status: CONFIRMED, ASSUMPTION, OPEN, or OUT OF SCOPE
- source ID
- owner
- effect if unresolved

Do not draft the proposal if scope, price, timeline, success criteria, client
dependencies, or approval owners remain unresolved. Instead, produce a concise
clarification list.

When the sheet is ready, draft:
1. client situation and desired outcome
2. proposed approach
3. deliverables and acceptance checks
4. client and provider responsibilities
5. exclusions
6. timeline with dependencies
7. approved pricing table copied exactly
8. assumptions and change process
9. next step

Add the supporting source ID after every material statement. End with a review
table for unsupported claims, contradictions, missing commitments, and numbers
that require independent verification.

Approved decision sheet:
[Paste de-identified decisions with source IDs and owners]
```

## Human review checklist

- The AI tool is approved for the information supplied.
- Client needs are represented accurately and not exaggerated.
- Every capability and credential is real and current.
- Deliverables are observable and acceptance checks are understandable.
- Included work, exclusions, revisions, and change handling are explicit.
- Client responsibilities and dependencies are visible.
- Dates reflect actual capacity and required review time.
- Every price, quantity, tax, expense, and payment condition was independently checked.
- Legal, insurance, privacy, security, and regulated requirements were reviewed by qualified owners.
- A person with authority approved the final proposal before sending it.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Proposal Readiness and Draft Pack**. The client supplies approved discovery notes, service options, pricing, and a proposal template. You deliver a decision sheet, clarification list, source-backed first draft, contradiction report, and final-review checklist.

You are organizing approved business decisions, not making legal commitments or choosing the client's price. Do not promise that a proposal will win work or produce a particular return.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one approved client brief into a decision sheet and source-backed proposal outline.
- **Medium:** Build reusable intake forms, service catalogs, pricing tables, approval gates, version control, and proposal quality checks.
- **Hard:** Design a governed proposal operation with CRM integration, controlled content libraries, role-based approvals, requirement matrices, security review, audit trails, analytics, and contract handoff.

## What's current

Recent 2026 proposal guidance describes AI as useful for converting discovery notes, approved content, and pricing data into tailored first drafts. The consistent warning is that people must still own capacity, scope, pricing, and final review.

Recent research on AI-generated business documents also highlights the consequence of fabricated prices or products in high-stakes applications. That makes the decision sheet valuable: it gives reviewers a compact place to find every commitment before polished prose hides an unsupported assumption.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional service and discovery call.
2. Build the decision sheet in a free document or spreadsheet.
3. Take Salesforce Trailhead's free proposal-writing lesson.
4. Use a free AI tier only with non-sensitive or properly de-identified inputs.
5. Compare the draft against the source and run the contradiction check manually.
6. Improve the template before paying for proposal automation software.

## The takeaway

AI can reduce blank-page work. The sellable skill is making the proposal reviewable: confirmed decisions go into the draft, unknowns become questions, and humans own every promise.
