# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's practical lesson is: **use AI to organize competitor evidence, not to invent competitor facts.**

Current guides warn that general AI prompts can confuse similarly named businesses, quote old prices, or compare companies serving different customers. A reliable comparison begins with a decision and current public sources.

## The 30-Minute Exercise

Choose one decision, such as: "Which service promises are common in my local market?"

Select three businesses that serve a similar customer need. For each one, collect the same public evidence:

- Official service page
- Official pricing page, if public
- Service area or location page
- FAQ or policy page
- Recent public reviews
- Date you checked each source

Do not collect private information, misrepresent yourself, bypass access controls, or copy another business's protected content.

## Copy This Prompt

```text
Compare the three businesses using only the source notes I provide.

Use these columns:
Business; Target customer; Public offer; Public price or "not listed";
Service area; Stated differentiator; Supporting source; Date checked.

Separate the output into:
1. Verified facts directly supported by a source
2. Inferences that require owner judgment
3. Unknowns that need more research

Do not estimate revenue, customers, profitability, quality, market share,
private strategy, or missing prices. Do not turn an inference into a fact.

Source notes:
[paste current notes and links]
```

## Verify Before Acting

- Open every linked page yourself.
- Check that prices, offers, and dates are current.
- Confirm that each business serves the same customer need.
- Treat reviews as individual experiences, not proof of universal quality.
- Turn findings into one small test for your own business, not imitation.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer a **Three-Competitor Evidence Brief**.

Deliverables: decision question, source table, verified comparison, inference list, unknowns, and three testable opportunities for the buyer to consider.

Learning curve: medium. Accurate source collection matters more than producing a large report. Avoid legal conclusions, deceptive research, unsupported market-size claims, and promises about revenue.

## Skill Ladder

- Easy: collect matching public pages and dates.
- Medium: compare consistent fields and verify changing facts.
- Hard: monitor market changes, quantify evidence quality, and integrate ethical competitive intelligence into planning.

## Takeaway

AI can sort the evidence. It cannot replace current sources or business judgment.
