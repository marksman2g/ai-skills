# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

AI can imitate a relative, manager, vendor, or public figure with convincing audio and video. Today’s lesson is not about learning to spot every fake. It is about using a verification process that still works when the voice sounds real.

## Today’s lesson: Verify the request, not the voice

Treat an unexpected request as unverified when it asks for:

- Money, gift cards, cryptocurrency, refunds, or changed payment details.
- Passwords, one-time codes, login links, or remote access.
- Customer, employee, financial, health, or other confidential information.
- Urgent secrecy or a demand to bypass the normal approval process.

Stop the conversation. Contact the person or organization through a phone number, app, website, or account you already trust. Do not use a number or link supplied in the suspicious message.

For a business, require a second person to approve unusual payments or changes to vendor banking details. A familiar voice, caller ID, email address, or video call should not replace the approval process.

## Try this in 20 minutes

Run a fictional verification drill with a family member or coworker.

1. Choose one scenario, such as an urgent request to pay a changed invoice.
2. Identify the trusted callback number or official account before the drill.
3. Write down which requests require a second approver.
4. Practice ending the incoming call or message without arguing.
5. Start a new contact using the trusted channel.
6. Confirm the request and the payment or account details separately.
7. Record who verified it, when, and how.
8. Save official fraud-reporting and financial-institution contact information.

Do not create or share a real person’s cloned voice for the exercise. Use plain text or role-play instead.

## Copy this safety card

```text
PAUSE: This request involves money, access, a code, or private information.

1. I will not act during this incoming call, message, or video.
2. I will not share a password or one-time verification code.
3. I will contact the person through a number or account I already trust.
4. I will verify payment or account changes using a second channel.
5. I will obtain the required second approval.
6. If verification fails, I will preserve the message and report it.

Urgency and a familiar voice are not proof of identity.
```

## Human review checklist

- Staff and family members know which requests trigger verification.
- Trusted callback details come from existing records, not the incoming message.
- One-time codes and passwords are never shared.
- Vendor payment changes receive independent confirmation.
- Unusual payments require a second approver.
- Verification is recorded before money or information moves.
- Suspected fraud is reported quickly to the bank, platform, employer, and appropriate authority.
- The process is practiced before an emergency occurs.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI Scam Verification Card Setup** for families or very small businesses. Help them document trusted callback channels, payment approval rules, escalation contacts, and a short practice drill. Do not claim to authenticate media, investigate crimes, recover funds, or provide cybersecurity or legal services unless qualified.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Create a one-page callback and verification card.
- **Medium:** Map payment, account-access, and confidential-data approval steps for a small team.
- **Hard:** Build a formal anti-fraud program with identity controls, dual approval, staff training, incident response, vendor verification, logging, and professional security review.

## The takeaway

You do not need to prove that a voice or video is fake before protecting yourself. Pause, leave the incoming channel, and verify the request through a trusted path you initiate.
