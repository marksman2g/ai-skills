Subject: Turn approved discovery notes into a safer AI-assisted proposal

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for drafting scope, deliverables, assumptions, and questions without inventing prices or promises.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

A client conversation often contains the raw material for a useful proposal: the problem, desired result, possible deliverables, constraints, and unanswered questions. AI can organize that material quickly, but polished writing can make an unsupported assumption look like an agreed commitment.

The valuable skill is not asking AI to "write a winning proposal." It is building a **traceable draft that separates confirmed facts from assumptions and decisions that still need approval**.

## Today's lesson: Give every commitment a status and source

Before drafting paragraphs, create a proposal facts table with these columns:

- client need or problem
- desired outcome
- confirmed deliverable
- exclusion or out-of-scope item
- assumption
- client responsibility
- dependency
- schedule status
- pricing status
- source reference
- approval status

Use five clear labels:

- **CONFIRMED:** Directly supported by approved notes or a source document.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A reasonable possibility that has not been agreed.
- **OPEN QUESTION:** Information required before the proposal can be finalized.
- **OWNER APPROVAL REQUIRED:** A business commitment that only an authorized owner may make.
- **CLIENT CONFIRMATION REQUIRED:** A detail the client must verify or accept.

AI may turn the table into readable prose. It should not silently upgrade an assumption into scope, a preference into a deadline, or a rough idea into a price.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use fictional notes or discovery notes you are authorized to process. Remove unnecessary personal, confidential, financial, or regulated information before using an AI tool.

1. Write the client's stated problem and desired result in plain language.
2. Extract every proposed deliverable, exclusion, dependency, client responsibility, date, price, and promise.
3. Attach a note timestamp, document section, email, or other approved source to each item.
4. Label each item `CONFIRMED`, `ASSUMPTION`, `OPEN QUESTION`, `OWNER APPROVAL REQUIRED`, or `CLIENT CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`.
5. Ask AI to identify contradictions, missing acceptance criteria, hidden dependencies, and unclear ownership.
6. Draft the proposal using only confirmed information.
7. Replace missing scope, dates, prices, and terms with `[TO BE CONFIRMED]` instead of guessing.
8. Add exclusions, client responsibilities, dependencies, revision limits, and an approval checklist.
9. Ask the delivery, finance, legal, privacy, or security owner to review the sections they control.
10. Share the document as a draft only after the authorized business owner approves the commitments.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious proposal-drafting assistant. Use only the approved discovery
notes and source documents provided below. Create a DRAFT for internal review.

First produce a proposal facts table with:
- client need and desired outcome
- confirmed deliverables
- exclusions and out-of-scope items
- assumptions and open questions
- client responsibilities and dependencies
- acceptance criteria
- schedule and pricing status
- source reference
- approval status

Label every item as CONFIRMED, ASSUMPTION, OPEN QUESTION,
OWNER APPROVAL REQUIRED, or CLIENT CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.

Then draft:
1. client situation and objective
2. proposed scope and deliverables
3. approach and milestones
4. client responsibilities and dependencies
5. exclusions
6. acceptance criteria
7. schedule, pricing, and payment placeholders
8. assumptions and open questions
9. approvals required before client delivery

Rules:
- do not invent pricing, fees, taxes, discounts, or payment terms
- do not invent deadlines, availability, staffing, or delivery capacity
- do not invent deliverables, warranties, guarantees, or promised outcomes
- do not create legal terms or imply authority to bind the business
- preserve important names, numbers, and wording exactly when confirmed
- write [TO BE CONFIRMED] wherever the source does not establish an answer
- flag contradictions and identify which owner must resolve each one
- do not describe the document as final, approved, or ready to send

Approved source material:
[Insert authorized discovery notes and documents]
```

## Human review checklist

- The notes and source files were authorized for AI processing.
- Sensitive or unnecessary information was removed or protected.
- Every material statement is traceable to a source or clearly labeled unconfirmed.
- Scope, deliverables, exclusions, acceptance criteria, and revision limits agree with one another.
- Prices, taxes, discounts, expenses, payment terms, and expiration dates were approved by the proper owner.
- Dates, staffing, availability, dependencies, and capacity were confirmed by the delivery owner.
- Client responsibilities and required access are explicit.
- Legal, privacy, security, procurement, accessibility, insurance, and compliance language was reviewed by qualified owners when relevant.
- No sentence guarantees an outcome the business cannot control.
- The authorized business owner approved the final version before it reached the client.

## What's current

Microsoft's current Word guidance says Copilot can start drafts from prompts, outlines, notes, templates, and referenced files. It can also structure content and add citations. Microsoft separately advises users to verify important facts, names, dates, numbers, recommendations, and sensitive statements before sharing or acting.

That supports a practical business rule: reference files help ground a proposal, but they do not turn every generated sentence into an approved commitment. Source checks, status labels, and owner approval are still part of the work.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional client and a free document editor or an AI tool you already have.
2. Build the facts table manually before asking AI to draft prose.
3. Compare every proposal sentence with its source and mark unsupported language.
4. Learn basic scope, acceptance criteria, exclusions, dependencies, and change control.
5. Watch the free official Microsoft walkthrough below to see source-based editing inside Word.
6. Use paid proposal, CRM, or automation tools only after the manual review-and-approval workflow works reliably.

## The takeaway

AI can make a proposal faster to organize and easier to read. The professional value comes from keeping facts traceable, gaps visible, commitments owner-approved, and the final document honest about what is still undecided.
