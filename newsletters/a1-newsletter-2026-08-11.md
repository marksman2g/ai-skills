# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's A1 AI lesson is:

**Teach AI when to stop.**

Current small-business AI guidance keeps repeating a practical pattern. AI can help with lead follow-up, customer support, inbox triage, scheduling, invoicing, reporting, research, and content. But the useful workflows all have the same safeguard:

```text
AI handles the first pass. A person handles exceptions.
```

That is the difference between a helpful workflow and a risky one.

If you are learning AI skills to sell, exception rules are a high-value beginner skill. They show that you understand more than prompts. You understand where business judgment matters.

## The Simple Lesson

An exception rule tells AI:

- When to continue
- When to flag something
- When to ask for more information
- When to stop completely
- Who should review the case

This matters because many business tasks are mostly repeatable, but not always repeatable.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act like an AI workflow safety designer for a small business.

I want to use AI for this repeated task:
[describe task]

Create exception rules that tell AI when to stop and ask a person.

Include:
1. Normal cases AI can help with
2. Warning signs AI should flag
3. Cases AI must not decide
4. Questions AI should ask when information is missing
5. Who should review the exception
6. A short reusable prompt with the stop rule included
7. A checklist before the final output is used
```

## Example

Task: customer support reply.

Normal cases AI can help with:

- Hours
- Basic service questions
- Appointment instructions
- Simple follow-up
- General FAQ answers

Warning signs:

- Angry customer
- Refund request
- Legal threat
- Medical, financial, or legal advice
- Custom pricing
- Private personal information
- Complaint about safety or harm
- Anything not covered by approved business facts

Stop rule:

```text
If the message includes a refund request, complaint, legal concern, sensitive personal information, emergency, custom pricing, or missing business policy, do not draft a final answer. Summarize the issue and flag it for the owner.
```

Reusable prompt:

```text
Use the approved business facts below to help with this customer message.

If this is a normal FAQ or scheduling question, draft a reply.
If this includes a refund, complaint, legal issue, emergency, sensitive personal information, custom pricing, or missing policy, stop and create an owner-review summary instead.

Customer message:
[paste message]

Approved business facts:
[paste facts]
```

## Why This Is Sellable

Small businesses want AI to save time, but they do not want AI creating preventable problems.

Exception rules help them:

- Avoid wrong promises
- Protect customer trust
- Reduce risky replies
- Train helpers faster
- Build confidence using AI
- Prepare for future automation or agents

This is a practical service because it improves safety before automation gets complicated.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer:

**AI Exception Rule Setup**

What you provide:

- One repeated task selected
- One normal-case list
- One exception list
- One stop-rule prompt
- One owner-review summary template
- One final review checklist
- Three example cases

Learning curve: easy to medium

Effort: low

Best buyer: small business owners, customer service teams, local service providers, nonprofits, creators, coaches, and any business using AI for replies or admin work.

Why people pay: they want AI speed without losing judgment.

## The Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Identify risky messages
- Write stop rules
- Create owner-review summaries
- Add a human approval checklist

Medium:

- Build exception rules for several workflows
- Create source-of-truth sheets
- Track what AI flags
- Improve prompts based on real cases

Hard:

- Build AI agents with approval gates
- Route exceptions to inboxes, task boards, or CRMs
- Log decisions and errors
- Monitor cost, accuracy, privacy, and adoption

The hard work can earn more, but the beginner version is the foundation.

## Try This Today

Pick one task:

- Customer reply
- Lead follow-up
- Appointment request
- Invoice reminder
- Inbox triage
- Social media comment response
- Quote request

Then write:

```text
Task:

Normal cases AI can help with:

AI must stop when:

AI should ask a question when:

Person who reviews exceptions:

Owner-review summary format:

Final checklist:
```

If you can write that for one task, you have a safer AI workflow.

## A1 Takeaway

Good AI workflows are not just about what AI can do.

They are about knowing when AI should stop.

That one rule can prevent most beginner mistakes.
