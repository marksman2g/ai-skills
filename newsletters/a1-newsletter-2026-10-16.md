Hello,

Realistic AI images are harder to judge by appearance alone. Today’s lesson replaces “look for weird hands” with an evidence-based process you can use before publishing, purchasing, reporting, or sharing.

## Today’s lesson: Check provenance, source, context, and corroboration

Use four layers of evidence:

1. **Original file:** Get the best available file instead of a screenshot or compressed repost.
2. **Provenance:** Check for signed Content Credentials or another supported watermark. Read what the result actually says about creation and editing.
3. **Source and context:** Find the earliest credible source and verify the claimed person, place, date, product, or event.
4. **Corroboration:** Use reverse image search and independent reliable sources to look for earlier versions or confirmation.

A missing credential does not prove an image is real. A valid credential records part of the file’s history; it does not prove every caption or claim about the scene is true. Detector percentages and visual oddities are leads, not final verdicts.

## Try this in 20 minutes

Practice with one public image that is not sensitive or private.

1. Save the page URL, publisher, caption, and date.
2. Download the best available file without editing it.
3. Make a separate working copy and preserve the original.
4. Open the original in the free Content Credentials verifier.
5. Record the signer, creation or editing claims, and verification status exactly as shown.
6. Run a reverse image search and locate the earliest credible appearance you can find.
7. Check whether independent sources support the caption’s event, date, and location.
8. Write one conclusion: `supported`, `contradicted`, or `not established by available evidence`.

Do not upload private, confidential, intimate, client-owned, or restricted images to public verification services without permission and an approved data-handling process.

## Human review checklist

- The best available original file was preserved unchanged.
- Provenance results were recorded exactly and their limits understood.
- The account, website, caption, date, and claimed event were checked separately.
- Reverse search results were opened rather than trusted from thumbnails.
- Independent sources support the claim, not merely copies of the same post.
- Visual oddities and detector scores are treated as clues.
- Uncertainty is stated clearly instead of forcing a yes-or-no answer.
- High-stakes decisions are escalated to qualified investigators or authorities.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Public Image Source Check** for low-risk marketing or publishing decisions. Deliver the original URL, provenance result, reverse-search findings, source timeline, corroborating links, and an uncertainty statement. Do not market this as forensic authentication, identity verification, copyright clearance, legal evidence, or fraud investigation.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Preserve the source, check Content Credentials, and run a reverse search.
- **Medium:** Build a documented source timeline and compare multiple independent reports.
- **Hard:** Perform professional media forensics using chain of custody, file analysis, provenance validation, geolocation, chronolocation, and qualified investigative review.

## The takeaway

The useful question is not only “Does this look AI-generated?” Ask where the file came from, what its provenance record says, whether the caption is supported, and what remains unknown.
