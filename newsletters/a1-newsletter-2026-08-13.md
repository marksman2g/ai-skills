# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's A1 AI lesson is:

**Build a failure plan before you trust an AI workflow.**

Current AI business coverage is moving past demos and into reliability. Recent reporting on trustworthy AI agents points out that many systems are judged by successful task completion, while the failed cases get less attention. For small businesses, that lesson is practical: do not only ask, "Can AI do this once?"

Ask:

```text
What happens when AI gets it wrong?
```

That question is where beginner AI work becomes business-ready.

## The Simple Lesson

Every AI workflow should have three safety pieces:

1. **Checkpoint**
   - Where the work can be reviewed before it continues.

2. **Failure log**
   - A simple place to record what went wrong.

3. **Recovery rule**
   - What to do next when AI fails, guesses, or gets stuck.

This does not need to be technical. A spreadsheet, note, or checklist is enough to start.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act like an AI workflow reliability coach for a small business.

I want to use AI for this repeated workflow:
[describe workflow]

Create a simple reliability plan with:
1. The normal workflow steps
2. Where checkpoints should happen
3. Common failure points
4. What to log when AI makes a mistake
5. A recovery rule for each failure
6. When a human must take over
7. A reusable failure-log template
8. A weekly review question to improve the workflow
```

## Example

Workflow: lead follow-up reply.

Normal steps:

1. New lead message arrives.
2. AI summarizes the request.
3. AI drafts a reply.
4. Human checks facts.
5. Human sends or edits the reply.
6. Follow-up task is saved.

Checkpoints:

- After AI summarizes the lead
- Before the reply is sent
- Before the follow-up task is saved

Common failures:

- AI guesses a service price
- AI misses a deadline
- AI uses the wrong tone
- AI overlooks missing information
- AI creates a follow-up task with no owner

Recovery rule:

```text
If AI guesses, stop and replace the guessed detail with a question for the business owner.
If AI misses a date, correct the date before the reply is sent.
If no owner is assigned, do not save the task until a person is named.
```

## Failure Log Template

Use this simple table:

```text
Date:
Workflow:
What AI was asked to do:
What went wrong:
Was it caught before customer use? yes/no
Human fix:
Prompt or process change needed:
Owner:
```

The goal is not to shame the tool. The goal is to improve the workflow.

## Why This Is Sellable

Small businesses want AI to save time, but reliability matters.

A failure plan helps them:

- Catch errors before customers see them
- Improve prompts over time
- Train helpers consistently
- Find workflows that are not ready for automation
- Build confidence before connecting AI to more tools

This is useful because many people are excited about AI agents, but they have not defined what happens when the agent fails.

## The Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Create a failure log
- Add checkpoints
- Write human takeover rules
- Improve prompts after mistakes

Medium:

- Review multiple workflows weekly
- Track error patterns
- Create exception rules
- Train a helper on reliability checks

Hard:

- Build AI agents with durable recovery
- Add scoped access to business tools
- Monitor logs, cost, accuracy, and privacy
- Design security and approval gates

The hard work can earn more, but the beginner version is enough to make AI safer and more useful.
