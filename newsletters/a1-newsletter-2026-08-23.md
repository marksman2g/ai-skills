# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's A1 AI lesson is:

**Control what AI can read and change.**

Current small-business AI news is moving from chat boxes into business software. This week, small-business updates covered AI in ads, campaign creation, finance tools, agents that can learn browser tasks, and AI systems that work with live business data.

That is useful, but it raises a practical question:

```text
What can AI read, what can it change, and who approves the result?
```

If a business cannot answer that question, it is not ready to let AI work inside important systems.

## The Simple Lesson

Before connecting AI to a business tool, define three permission levels:

1. **Read**
   - What information can AI look at?

2. **Prepare**
   - What can AI draft, summarize, classify, compare, or recommend?

3. **Change**
   - What can AI update, send, publish, purchase, delete, or approve?

For beginner workflows, keep AI in the first two levels:

```text
AI can read approved information.
AI can prepare the next step.
A person approves anything that changes the business record or reaches a customer.
```

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act like an AI permissions reviewer for a small business.

I want to use AI in this workflow:
[describe workflow]

Create a permissions plan with:
1. What AI may read
2. What AI may prepare
3. What AI may not change
4. What requires human approval
5. What data should never be pasted into AI
6. What action should be logged
7. What exception should stop the workflow
8. A simple prompt that keeps AI within these limits
```

## Example

Workflow: AI helps with customer follow-up.

AI may read:

- Customer message
- Approved service list
- Approved price language
- Business hours
- Booking link

AI may prepare:

- Summary of customer request
- Missing information list
- Draft reply
- Suggested follow-up task

AI may not change:

- Customer record
- Price
- Appointment time
- Refund status
- Invoice
- Published website content
- Final message sent to customer

Human approval required before:

- Sending reply
- Updating CRM
- Confirming price
- Scheduling appointment
- Offering discount
- Making any promise

That is a safe first version.

## Why This Is Sellable

Small businesses are being offered more AI tools inside apps they already use.

That creates value, but also risk.

A permissions plan helps a business:

- Avoid accidental changes
- Protect customer data
- Keep owners in control
- Use AI without guessing
- Train helpers faster
- Prepare for agents later
- Decide what should stay manual

This is a practical AI skill because it turns vague AI use into controlled workflow design.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer:

**AI Permissions and Approval Map**

What you provide:

- One workflow selected
- Read permissions list
- Prepare permissions list
- No-change list
- Human approval checklist
- Private-data warning list
- Exception rule
- One reusable prompt

Learning curve: easy to medium

Effort: low

Best buyer: small business owners, ecommerce sellers, creators, accountants, local service providers, nonprofits, coaches, and teams considering AI tools that connect to ads, finance, inboxes, calendars, or CRM systems.

Why people pay: they want AI help without giving it too much authority.

## The Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Define what AI can read
- Define what AI can draft
- Add human approval before sending
- Create a private-data warning list

Medium:

- Map permissions across several workflows
- Add logs for AI-assisted actions
- Create exception rules
- Compare tool privacy settings

Hard:

- Connect AI agents to business systems
- Add role-based permissions
- Track changes, approvals, cost, privacy, and errors
- Build agent workflows that safely update live systems

The hard work can earn more, but permissions are the foundation.

As AI moves into business software, the important skill is not just prompting.
