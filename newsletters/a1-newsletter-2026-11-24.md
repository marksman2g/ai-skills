Subject: Turn a refund request into a clear evidence pack with AI

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for organizing receipts, policies, messages, and missing facts without letting AI decide the refund.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Refund requests can become confusing quickly. The customer remembers one promise, the receipt shows another date, the website policy may have changed, and several messages may contain different details. AI can organize the record, but it should not decide eligibility, interpret the law, alter a transaction, or accuse anyone of dishonesty.

The practical skill is an **AI-drafted refund evidence pack**: create a neutral timeline, connect every important statement to a source, identify missing information, quote the applicable policy, and prepare a response for authorized human review.

## Today's lesson: Separate evidence, claims, and decisions

Keep three columns:

- **Verified evidence:** What an original receipt, policy, order record, warranty, or message actually shows.
- **Customer or business statement:** What someone says happened, preserved without treating it as proven or false.
- **Decision required:** The policy, authority, specialist, or missing fact needed before anyone responds.

AI may help organize those columns. The current policy and authorized reviewer determine the outcome.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional case or records you are authorized to process in an approved AI environment. Remove full card numbers, bank details, passwords, PINs, and unrelated personal information.

1. Preserve the original receipt, invoice, order record, warranty, policy, advertisement, and correspondence.
2. Create an evidence index with source, date, version, page or screenshot, and a short description.
3. Build a timeline of purchase, delivery, complaint, promised actions, and follow-ups.
4. Record what resolution the customer requested without assuming entitlement.
5. Quote the policy language that may apply and retain its effective date or version.
6. Separate verified facts from customer statements, staff statements, and AI inferences.
7. Mark missing documents, conflicting dates, unclear terms, and required specialist review.
8. Ask AI to draft a calm acknowledgment that does not promise or deny a refund.
9. Have an authorized person apply the policy and decide the outcome.
10. Record the decision, reason, approver, communication, and any transaction action separately.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious refund-case organizer. Use only the authorized records below.
Create a DRAFT evidence pack and neutral response for human review.

Return:
1. evidence index with source, date, version, and relevant page or message
2. chronological timeline
3. customer-requested resolution
4. verified facts with citations
5. customer and staff statements, labeled as statements
6. exact policy, warranty, or advertised language that may apply
7. missing or conflicting information
8. questions for the authorized reviewer
9. a neutral acknowledgment that confirms review, not an outcome

Rules:
- do not approve, refuse, promise, issue, reverse, or dispute a refund
- do not alter an order, customer record, or transaction
- do not interpret law, policy eligibility, or payment-network rules
- do not accuse anyone of fraud, deception, theft, abuse, or dishonesty
- do not infer intent or invent facts, deadlines, quotations, or evidence
- preserve original wording and cite every important claim
- label AI-written text DRAFT
- show REVIEW REQUIRED when authority or specialist judgment is needed

Current approved policy:
[Insert the authorized policy and version]

Case records:
[Insert the authorized, privacy-reviewed records]
```

## Human review checklist

- Original records are preserved and the evidence index points to them.
- The policy version was in effect for the relevant purchase or event.
- Receipts, dates, amounts, product or service details, and correspondence are accurate.
- Advertised promises, warranties, exclusions, fees, and deadlines are quoted in context.
- Statements are not mislabeled as verified facts.
- Missing evidence and conflicting records remain visible.
- The response is calm, specific, and does not promise an unauthorized result.
- Payment-method, chargeback, legal, tax, privacy, accessibility, and safety issues reached qualified reviewers.
- Sensitive payment and identity data stayed out of unapproved AI tools.
- The authorized person made and documented the decision.
- Any refund, reversal, dispute, or account change occurred outside the AI draft workflow.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI-Assisted Refund Evidence Pack** for a small batch of low-risk cases. Deliver an evidence index, timeline, fact-and-statement table, policy excerpt, missing-information list, reviewer queue, neutral response draft, and decision-record template.

The client supplies current policies, authoritative systems, secure access, specialist reviewers, refund authority, and final decisions. You organize evidence and prepare drafts; you do not decide eligibility, alter transactions, investigate fraud, provide legal or financial advice, or communicate without approval.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Organize one low-risk case with a receipt, policy, and a few messages. Learning curve: several practice cases with fictional records and supervised review.
- **Medium:** Build reusable evidence indexes, policy-version controls, templates, escalation rules, privacy checks, response libraries, and decision logs across channels. Learning curve: several weeks learning the client's products, policies, systems, and approval rules.
- **Hard:** Design governed returns and dispute operations with payment integrations, identity and fraud controls, jurisdiction-aware policy management, quality monitoring, audit trails, accessibility, analytics, and human appeals. Learning curve: months plus customer-service, payments, legal, privacy, security, and systems specialists.

## What's current

FTC consumer guidance recommends keeping receipts, warranties, invoices, contracts, statements, correspondence, dates, representative names, and promised actions. It also recommends describing the problem and requested resolution calmly and specifically while preserving original records.

FTC online-shopping guidance reminds buyers to check the seller's actual return policy, deadlines, shipping responsibility, restocking fees, and special rules. There is no single universal return rule that an AI assistant should assume.

CFPB guidance explains that payment disputes and chargeback options depend on the payment method, facts, agreement, and timing. That is why the AI draft should point the reader to the seller and payment provider's current process rather than promise a chargeback or reuse one deadline for every case.

## The takeaway

AI can make a refund case easier to understand. A fair outcome still depends on authentic records, the correct policy version, calm communication, privacy, specialist review, and an authorized human decision.
