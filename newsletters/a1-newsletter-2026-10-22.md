# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

AI can produce a fluent translation in seconds. Fluent does not always mean accurate, appropriate, or ready to send. Today’s lesson shows how to use AI for a low-risk first draft while keeping people responsible for the final message.

## Today’s lesson: Route translation work by consequence

Classify the material before choosing a workflow:

- **Low risk:** Informal internal updates, early drafts, or reversible messages may use AI with human review.
- **Customer-facing:** Websites, support messages, instructions, and marketing need review by someone fluent in the target language and familiar with the audience.
- **High stakes:** Legal, medical, financial, safety, immigration, employment, contractual, eligibility, or deadline-sensitive material needs a qualified human translator or specialist process from the start.

Prepare the source in plain language before translating. Ambiguous English becomes ambiguous translation. Use a short glossary for product names, approved terms, acronyms, units, and words that must remain unchanged.

## Try this in 25 minutes

Practice with a fictional, low-risk appointment reminder or store-hours notice.

1. Name the audience, language, region, and purpose.
2. Simplify the source without changing its meaning.
3. Create a glossary for names, dates, times, prices, units, links, and required terms.
4. Ask an approved AI tool for a first translation draft.
5. Have a different tool or person translate the result back into the source language.
6. Compare the back-translation with the original meaning.
7. Ask a fluent target-language reviewer to check accuracy, tone, dialect, and cultural fit.
8. Test links, phone support, layout, line breaks, characters, and the message in its final context.

Back-translation is a useful check, not final approval. Two systems can repeat the same error, and a word-for-word match can still sound unnatural to the intended audience.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious translation drafting assistant. Translate the approved low-risk text

number, unit, link, condition, warning, and required term. Follow the glossary exactly.

Use natural plain language for the target audience. Do not invent information, expand
```

## Human review checklist

- The material was classified by the consequence of an error.
- Confidential or personal information stayed out of unapproved tools.
- The source was clear before translation began.
- Names, numbers, dates, prices, units, links, and conditions were preserved.
- A glossary controls important terminology.
- Idioms, slang, dialect, tone, and cultural context were reviewed.
- A fluent target-language reviewer checked the final message in context.
- High-stakes content was routed to qualified human professionals.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Low-Risk Translation Preparation Pack**. Help a business classify content, simplify the approved source, build a glossary, organize AI draft and back-translation comparisons, and prepare questions for a fluent reviewer. Do not sell the output as certified, legal, medical, safety, or professional translation unless qualified people perform the required work.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Translate one low-risk notice with a glossary and back-translation check.
- **Medium:** Build reusable terminology, review forms, regional style guidance, and in-context quality checks.
- **Hard:** Manage multilingual content with professional linguists, translation memory, localization engineering, accessibility, legal review, security controls, quality measurement, and publication approval.

## The takeaway

AI translation is a drafting method, not a quality guarantee. Match the review process to the harm a translation error could cause.
