Subject: Turn an approved meeting transcript into an action register

Preview text: A 25-minute AI lesson for finding decisions, owners, deadlines, and unresolved questions without inventing commitments.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

AI meeting summaries can save time, but a polished recap is not automatically an accurate work plan. A speaker may suggest an idea without accepting responsibility. A date may be discussed without becoming a deadline. A transcription error may even attach a task to the wrong person.

The useful skill is converting an approved transcript into a **proposed action register**, then asking the people involved to confirm it.

## Today's lesson: Separate discussion from commitment

Give every extracted item one of four labels:

- **Decision:** The group clearly selected an option or approved a direction.
- **Action:** Someone explicitly agreed to complete a task.
- **Open question:** The issue was discussed but not resolved.
- **Suggestion:** An idea was raised without approval or ownership.

For every decision or action, save the speaker, transcript timestamp, exact supporting words, proposed owner, proposed deadline, and confirmation status. If the transcript does not support an owner or date, leave the field blank.

## Try this in 25 minutes

Use a fictional meeting or a transcript that your organization has approved for this purpose. Follow the meeting's notice, consent, access, retention, and confidentiality requirements.

1. Copy the approved transcript into an approved AI workspace.
2. Ask AI to classify decisions, actions, open questions, and suggestions separately.
3. Require a timestamp and short source excerpt for every extracted item.
4. Put the results in a free spreadsheet with columns for item, type, source, owner, deadline, status, and confirmation.
5. Replace guessed names or dates with `UNCONFIRMED`.
6. Compare every row with the transcript and correct speaker-identification errors.
7. Send each proposed action to its named owner for confirmation.
8. Move confirmed tasks into the team's normal task system.
9. Keep unresolved questions visible instead of disguising them as decisions.
10. Store or delete the transcript, summary, and register according to the organization's rules.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious meeting follow-up assistant. Use only the approved transcript
below. Produce a PROPOSED action register for human confirmation.

Classify each item as:
- DECISION: clearly approved or selected
- ACTION: a person clearly accepted a task
- OPEN QUESTION: discussed but unresolved
- SUGGESTION: proposed without approval or ownership

For every item include:
- concise description
- speaker
- transcript timestamp
- short supporting excerpt
- proposed owner, or UNCONFIRMED
- proposed deadline, or UNCONFIRMED
- dependencies
- confidence: high, medium, or low
- confirmation status: NOT YET CONFIRMED

Do not infer agreement from silence. Do not turn suggestions into decisions. Do
not assign a task because someone discussed it. Do not calculate or invent a
deadline. Flag conflicting statements, transcription uncertainty, and missing
context. Finish with a list of questions that must be answered by people.

Approved transcript:
[Insert the authorized transcript]
```

## Human review checklist

- Everyone received any notice or consent required for recording, transcription, and AI processing.
- The AI workspace is approved for the meeting's information sensitivity.
- Each decision and action has a traceable transcript source.
- Speaker names have been checked against the recording or meeting participants.
- Suggestions, tentative language, and unresolved issues are not presented as commitments.
- No owner or deadline was invented from context.
- Every proposed owner has confirmed the task, scope, and due date.
- Confidential, personal, legal, financial, health, employee, and customer information receives the required handling.
- The final register is stored in the team's approved system of record.
- Transcript, recording, summary, and AI output follow applicable retention and deletion rules.

## What's current

Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, and Zoom now offer AI-assisted summaries or action items in supported plans. Their own guidance still makes the review boundary important: Microsoft tells users to verify AI-generated meeting results, Google warns that generated notes can be incomplete or inaccurate, and Microsoft says AI audio recaps should be checked for accuracy.

These tools also depend on platform settings and source availability. For example, Microsoft explains that post-meeting Copilot answers can use a transcript when one exists, while Google stores generated Meet notes in the organizer's Drive and applies the organization's retention policy.

The practical lesson is simple: use the automation to find possible work, but use source evidence and human confirmation to create real assignments.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional five-minute transcript and a free spreadsheet.
2. Learn to distinguish decisions, actions, questions, and suggestions before adding automation.
3. Require timestamps and evidence in every practice run.
4. Test difficult cases: vague ownership, relative dates, speaker errors, conflicting statements, and tentative language.
5. Create a short confirmation message for owners: task, due date, source, and approve-or-correct buttons or choices.
6. Pay for meeting intelligence features only after the manual review workflow works reliably.

## The takeaway

AI can draft the follow-up. People must confirm who promised what, by when, and whether a discussion actually became a decision.
