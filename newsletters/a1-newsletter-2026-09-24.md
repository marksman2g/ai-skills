# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's practical AI lesson is: **do not publish an important AI-generated claim until you can connect it to a real source.**

AI can produce polished answers quickly, but speed and confidence are not proof. Recent verification guides recommend checking whether a cited source exists, locating the exact supporting passage, recalculating important numbers, and reading the surrounding context.

## Build a Claim-to-Source Ledger

Copy every important claim into a four-column table:

```text
CLAIM | SOURCE LINK | SUPPORTING PASSAGE | STATUS
```

Use only three status labels:

- Verified: the source directly supports the claim.
- Revise: the source supports only a narrower claim.
- Remove: the source is missing, irrelevant, outdated, or contradictory.

Always check names, dates, numbers, calculations, quotations, links, credentials, and claims about laws, prices, products, health, safety, or money.

## Copy This Prompt

```text
Review the draft below as a verification assistant.

Extract every checkable claim, including names, dates, numbers,
calculations, quotations, links, credentials, comparisons, and recommendations.

Return a table with:
1. Exact claim
2. What evidence would verify it
3. Risk if wrong: low, medium, or high
4. Verification status: unverified

Do not invent citations or mark anything verified.
Draft:
[paste the draft]
```

The prompt creates the checklist. A person must open the original sources and complete the verification.

## Fifteen-Minute Exercise

Take one AI-assisted paragraph. Highlight every factual claim. Verify the three highest-risk claims using original publishers, official documentation, government pages, research papers, or the organization responsible for the information.

If a source says something similar but not identical, narrow the wording. If you cannot find support, remove the claim or label it clearly as an assumption.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer an **AI Content Verification Check** for low-risk business articles, FAQs, and educational posts.

Deliverables: marked claims, source ledger, corrected links, revised wording, and a list of unresolved items. Learning curve: easy to medium. Do not offer legal, medical, financial, or compliance verification without appropriate qualifications.

## Skill Ladder

- Easy: check links, names, dates, and quotations.
- Medium: compare multiple primary sources and recalculate figures.
- Hard: design documented review systems for regulated or high-consequence work with qualified experts.

## Takeaway

AI can help identify what needs checking. Evidence decides what is true enough to use.
