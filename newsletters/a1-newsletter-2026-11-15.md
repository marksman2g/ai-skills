Subject: Turn one process demonstration into a reviewable SOP with AI

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for converting approved notes or a transcript into clear steps without inventing rules, permissions, or safety instructions.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Many businesses depend on processes that live in one person's memory. Someone knows how to prepare the weekly report, open the store, update a customer record, or hand off a project, but the steps are scattered across habits, notes, and conversations.

AI can turn a demonstration or transcript into a useful first draft. The valuable skill is creating a **reviewable standard operating procedure**, not declaring an AI summary official before the process owner checks it.

## Today's lesson: Separate what was observed from what was confirmed

Build the draft with four evidence labels:

- **Observed:** The step appeared in the approved demonstration or transcript.
- **Owner confirmed:** The process owner explicitly verified the step or rule.
- **Needs clarification:** The source was vague, incomplete, or contradictory.
- **Not demonstrated:** The SOP needs information that was absent, such as exceptions or recovery steps.

Every important step should point to a timestamp, screenshot, approved note, form, or policy. AI can organize the evidence, but the owner decides what the procedure actually requires.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Choose a short, low-risk task that you are authorized to document. Avoid credentials, payment approval, regulated decisions, hazardous work, or sensitive customer information while learning.

1. Define the process name, purpose, audience, owner, start point, and finish point.
2. Record an approved demonstration or collect the owner's rough notes.
3. Transcribe the explanation with timestamps and correct obvious speaker or terminology errors.
4. Ask AI to extract steps, inputs, outputs, tools, decisions, warnings, exceptions, and unanswered questions.
5. Require a source reference for each extracted item.
6. Draft the SOP with numbered steps and separate sections for prerequisites, checks, exceptions, and escalation.
7. Mark any inferred step as `NEEDS OWNER CONFIRMATION`.
8. Ask the process owner to perform the task using only the draft.
9. Record every place where the owner pauses, corrects the document, or relies on unstated knowledge.
10. Update the SOP, obtain approval, assign a review date, and preserve the source and version history.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious process-documentation assistant. Use only the approved source
material below. Create a DRAFT SOP for review; do not present it as approved.

For each step include:
- step number and action
- responsible role
- required input or tool
- expected result
- verification check
- source timestamp or reference
- evidence status: OBSERVED, OWNER CONFIRMED, NEEDS CLARIFICATION,
  or NOT DEMONSTRATED

Create sections for:
1. purpose and scope
2. owner, audience, prerequisites, and permissions
3. numbered procedure
4. decision points and exceptions
5. safety, privacy, quality, and security warnings found in the source
6. failure recovery and escalation
7. completion evidence
8. unanswered questions
9. approval and review history

Rules:
- do not invent credentials, permissions, policies, deadlines, warnings, or approvals
- do not convert one observed workaround into an official rule
- preserve important numbers, names, labels, and system fields exactly
- identify steps that depend on judgment or hidden knowledge
- flag contradictions and missing exception paths
- state when the source does not establish an answer

Approved source material:
[Insert the authorized transcript, notes, screenshots, and policies]
```

## Human review checklist

- The demonstration and source files were authorized for recording and AI processing.
- Secrets, customer information, employee data, and unrelated screen content were excluded or protected.
- Every important step has a source reference or owner confirmation.
- The draft distinguishes observed actions from official rules.
- Roles, permissions, tools, inputs, outputs, and completion checks are accurate.
- Exceptions, error recovery, escalation, and stop conditions are documented.
- Safety, legal, privacy, security, financial, and regulated requirements come from qualified owners.
- A second person successfully tested the process using only the draft.
- The owner approved the final version and assigned a review date.
- Previous versions and source evidence remain available according to the organization's records rules.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one approved five-to-ten-minute demonstration into a sourced draft SOP and test it with the owner.
- **Medium:** Build templates, version control, role assignments, review calendars, linked forms, exception libraries, training checklists, and change-request workflows.
- **Hard:** Design an enterprise process-management system with access controls, policy mapping, risk classification, approvals, audit trails, training records, automated controls, process mining, testing, and monitored AI-agent execution.

## What's current

Microsoft's current Word guidance says Copilot can draft from notes, outlines, templates, and referenced files, add structured content, and generate citations. Microsoft also tells users to verify facts, numbers, names, policy statements, and other sensitive details before sharing.

Word's transcription feature can separate speakers, retain timestamps, replay the source audio, and insert transcript snippets into a document. Those timestamps are useful evidence anchors when a reviewer needs to check how a step was described.

Microsoft also published a recent 2026 guide specifically for creating SOP templates with AI. The important extra control is to test the finished draft with the process owner: a well-formatted procedure can still omit the small decisions that experienced workers perform automatically.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional task or a simple personal process that contains no sensitive information.
2. Record a short voice explanation using a device or tool you already have.
3. Use a free document template with purpose, prerequisites, steps, checks, exceptions, and approval sections.
4. Add timestamps manually if free transcription is unavailable.
5. Ask another person to follow the SOP without coaching and record every gap.
6. Pay for transcription or document AI only after the manual review-and-test loop works reliably.

## The takeaway

AI can turn experience into a fast first draft. A trustworthy SOP still needs traceable evidence, hidden-knowledge questions, a real test run, owner approval, and a date for the next review.
