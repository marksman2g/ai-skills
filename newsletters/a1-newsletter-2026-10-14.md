Hello,

Images can inform, decorate, link, or display complex information. Today’s lesson shows how AI can help draft alternative text while a person decides what the image means in its specific context.

## Today’s lesson: Describe the image’s purpose, not every pixel

Before writing alt text, classify the image:

1. **Informative image:** Briefly describe the meaning the image adds to the page.
2. **Functional image:** Describe what the linked image, icon, or button does.
3. **Decorative or redundant image:** Use empty alt text when the image adds no information or repeats nearby text.
4. **Image containing important text:** Include the necessary text if it is not available elsewhere.
5. **Complex chart or diagram:** Provide the key takeaway and make the full information available in nearby text or another accessible format.

AI can identify visible objects, but it may miss why the image appears on that page, invent details, misread text, or overdescribe unimportant features. Context and human review are essential.

## Try this in 20 minutes

Choose five images from a website, document, or social post that you are authorized to review.

1. Read the surrounding heading, caption, and paragraph.
2. Classify each image using the five categories above.
3. Write one sentence explaining what a person would miss without the image.
4. Ask an approved AI tool for a first draft using the image and page context.
5. Remove guesses, unnecessary visual detail, and repeated nearby text.
6. Check names, visible text, products, locations, and actions against the source.
7. Preview the published page or document and confirm the alternative works in context.

Do not upload confidential, private, licensed, or client-owned images to an AI service unless that tool and use are approved.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as an accessibility drafting assistant. I will provide an authorized image and the
verified text surrounding it. First classify the image as informative, functional,
decorative/redundant, text-containing, or complex. Explain the classification briefly.

Then draft concise alt text that communicates the image's purpose in this context.
For a decorative or fully redundant image, recommend empty alt text. For a functional
image, describe the action or destination. For a complex chart, draft a short summary
and list the information that must also appear in accessible text nearby.

Do not guess identity, race, ethnicity, disability, gender, emotion, location, product,
visible text, numbers, or events. Mark uncertain details [VERIFY]. Do not add keywords
for search visibility. A human editor will make the final decision.

Page purpose and surrounding text:
[Insert approved context]
```

## Human review checklist

- The image’s purpose was decided from the page context.
- Decorative and redundant images are not given noisy descriptions.
- Linked images and icons describe their action or destination.
- Important visible text is available to nonvisual users.
- Charts and diagrams have accessible underlying information.
- Names, numbers, products, places, and actions were verified.
- Guesses about identity, emotion, or personal traits were removed.
- The result was checked in the actual page or document, not only in the AI chat.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI-Assisted Image Description Review** for a defined set of website or document images. Deliver an inventory, image classification, human-reviewed alt-text drafts, and a list of complex images needing longer descriptions. Do not promise full accessibility or legal compliance from an alt-text review alone.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Classify and review five simple images using the W3C decision tree.
- **Medium:** Create an image inventory and a consistent description style for a site or document library.
- **Hard:** Perform broader accessibility remediation with screen-reader testing, complex-image alternatives, accessible code and documents, user testing, and qualified accessibility oversight.

## The takeaway

AI can help overcome the blank page, but it cannot reliably decide what an image means to a user in context. Use it for a draft, then make the accessibility decision yourself.
