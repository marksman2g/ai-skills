# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Many small businesses already have useful material: a service page, FAQ, customer-approved story, event notice, product sheet, or recorded explanation. The problem is turning that material into consistent social content without spending hours rewriting it.

AI can help repurpose one approved source into several first drafts. The marketable skill is not pressing "generate." It is preserving the facts, adapting the message for each platform, adding a real human point of view, and giving the client an organized review package.

## Today's lesson: Build a source-backed content pack

Start with one current source the business owns or has permission to use. Create a small source sheet before prompting AI:

- **Goal:** What should the content help the audience understand or do?
- **Audience:** Who is the post for, and what problem do they recognize?
- **Approved facts:** Which services, dates, prices, features, locations, and policies are confirmed?
- **Proof:** What source supports each factual or performance claim?
- **Voice:** Which three words describe the business's normal tone?
- **Restrictions:** What must not be promised, disclosed, or published?
- **Call to action:** What is the one approved next step?
- **Owner:** Who must approve the final content?

Then adapt the idea instead of copying the same text everywhere. For example, make a concise Facebook post, an Instagram caption with a visual concept, a LinkedIn lesson, and a 30-second vertical-video script.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use your own material or a fictional business. Do not use confidential client information.

1. Choose one current, approved source and record its URL, owner, and date.
2. Highlight only the facts, quotes, and calls to action you may reuse.
3. Build a simple claim table with columns for **Claim**, **Source**, **Status**, and **Reviewer**.
4. Ask AI for four platform-specific drafts using only the approved material.
5. Require AI to label unsupported statements instead of filling gaps.
6. Add one real observation, opinion, example, or behind-the-scenes detail from the business owner.
7. Check every name, date, number, offer, link, testimonial, and image right or permission.
8. Place the drafts in a calendar with the platform, format, goal, approval status, and publication date.
9. Send the pack to the owner for approval before scheduling or publishing anything.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a careful social-content assistant. Use only the approved source material
below. Do not invent facts, customer experiences, quotes, prices, discounts,
deadlines, product capabilities, performance results, legal claims, or statistics.

Create four distinct drafts:
1. a Facebook post for conversation and local relevance
2. an Instagram caption plus a simple visual concept
3. a LinkedIn post that teaches one useful business lesson
4. a 30-second vertical-video script with hook, three beats, and call to action

For each draft, include:
- audience and purpose
- source IDs for every factual claim
- suggested headline or opening hook
- platform-appropriate wording and length
- one approved call to action
- accessibility notes, including useful alt-text details when relevant
- a list of facts or permissions that still need human confirmation

Keep the business voice [three voice words]. Do not imitate a living creator.
Do not add hashtags unless they are relevant and understandable. Mark every
unsupported idea "DO NOT PUBLISH - NEEDS CONFIRMATION." End with a claim table.

Approved source material:
[Paste de-identified source text with source IDs]
```

## Human review checklist

- The business owns the source or has permission to reuse it.
- Every factual claim points to a current approved source.
- Quotes and testimonials are real, authorized, and not edited misleadingly.
- Material relationships, sponsorships, or incentives are clearly disclosed when required.
- The draft does not promise earnings, guaranteed results, health outcomes, or unsupported superiority.
- Names, dates, prices, locations, offers, and links are correct.
- Each platform version fits its audience instead of repeating identical copy.
- A human added the business's real experience, judgment, or point of view.
- Images, music, logos, and clips have appropriate rights and permissions.
- The content is accessible and has a clear owner approval before publication.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Source-Backed Content Repurposing Pack**. The client provides one approved source and brand notes. You deliver four reviewed platform drafts, one short-video script, a visual brief, a claim-and-source table, a one-week calendar, and one revision after client review.

Begin with drafting and organization, not automatic publishing. Use fictional samples until you can demonstrate accuracy, reliable turnaround, and a clean approval process. Price the work according to scope, review effort, and local market conditions rather than promising a particular income.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one approved source into four clearly labeled first drafts and a one-week calendar.
- **Medium:** Build reusable brand-voice guides, client intake forms, claim tables, approval workflows, performance reviews, and monthly content systems.
- **Hard:** Design a governed multi-channel content operation with asset rights, audience research, experimentation, analytics, automation, security, records, and legal or compliance review.

## What's current

HubSpot's 2026 marketing survey reports that social media remains a major marketing channel, media creation is a common AI use, and repurposing content across channels is a leading trend. Its reporting also warns that generic AI content is becoming less effective and that a distinct brand point of view matters. The practical opportunity is therefore not unlimited content volume; it is efficient adaptation with stronger source material and human judgment.

The FTC's standing guidance remains equally important: advertising claims must be truthful, not deceptive or unfair, and supported by evidence. Endorsements must reflect honest experience, and material connections that could affect how audiences evaluate an endorsement should be disclosed.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with one page from your own website or a fictional business.
2. Track claims and approvals in a free spreadsheet or document.
3. Use a free AI tier with public or de-identified material only.
4. Build the calendar in a free spreadsheet before paying for scheduling software.
5. Learn visual planning with Canva's free tutorials and create only media you have permission to use.
6. Review performance manually and improve one variable at a time before adding automation.

## The takeaway

The easiest content service to sell responsibly is not "AI will run your social media." It is a bounded, reviewable deliverable: one approved source becomes several platform-ready drafts, and every important claim remains traceable to evidence.
