Subject: Turn one repeated task into a tested SOP with AI

Preview text: A 30-minute workflow for converting a real process into clear instructions without letting AI invent missing steps or hide exceptions.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Small businesses often depend on knowledge that lives in one person's head. When that person is unavailable, routine work slows down or gets completed differently every time.

AI can turn notes or a narrated demonstration into a first-draft standard operating procedure. The valuable skill is verifying that the written procedure matches the real work and can be followed by someone else.

## Today's lesson: Document one outcome from start to finish

Choose a low-risk, repeatable task with a clear result, such as preparing a weekly update, organizing approved files, or publishing a reviewed social post.

A useful SOP should answer:

- **Purpose:** What result should this procedure produce?
- **Scope:** When should it be used, and when should it not?
- **Owner:** Who maintains and approves it?
- **Inputs:** What information, access, and tools are required?
- **Steps:** What actions happen, in order?
- **Decisions:** What conditions change the path?
- **Checks:** How does the worker know each important step succeeded?
- **Exceptions:** When must the worker stop or escalate?
- **Evidence:** What record proves the task was completed?
- **Version:** When was the procedure approved, tested, and last reviewed?

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional workflow or an approved low-risk business task.

1. Perform the task while taking notes or recording the approved screen and narration.
2. Remove passwords, personal data, confidential material, customer records, and unnecessary access details.
3. Give each source note or recording segment a stable timestamp or ID.
4. Ask AI to draft the SOP using only those sources.
5. Require a source reference for every step, warning, decision, and completion check.
6. Mark anything mentioned but not demonstrated as **Needs owner confirmation**.
7. Ask the person who performs the work to correct the draft.
8. Give the procedure to someone unfamiliar with the task and observe where they pause, guess, or fail.
9. Update the SOP, record the test result, assign an owner, and set a review date.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious process-documentation assistant. Use only the approved source notes
or transcript below. Do not invent steps, permissions, credentials, safety rules,
business policy, owners, deadlines, exceptions, or completion criteria.

Create a draft SOP with:
- title and document ID
- purpose, scope, and exclusions
- owner and required approvals
- prerequisites, inputs, tools, and access
- numbered steps with one observable outcome per step
- decision points written as if/then rules
- warnings, stop conditions, and escalation path
- quality checks and final completion evidence
- common errors and recovery steps supported by the source
- version, effective date, review date, and change log

For every important instruction, include the source timestamp or note ID. Label
information that was mentioned but not demonstrated "Needs owner confirmation."
List missing details and conflicting instructions separately. End with a short test
script that a second person can use to validate the SOP.

Approved source material:
[Paste de-identified notes or timestamped transcript]
```

## Human review checklist

- The SOP describes one defined outcome rather than several unrelated tasks.
- The source material is preserved for comparison.
- Each important step has evidence in the source.
- Required access uses roles, not exposed passwords or secrets.
- Decision points, exceptions, and stop conditions are explicit.
- Safety, legal, financial, employment, and security instructions came from qualified owners.
- Every step has an observable result or checkpoint.
- A second person tested the procedure without informal coaching.
- Test failures and corrections appear in the change log.
- A named owner and review date keep the procedure current.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Process-to-SOP Starter Pack**. The client chooses one approved low-risk process and demonstrates it. You deliver the source transcript, draft SOP, missing-information list, test checklist, change log, and one revision after a staff member tests it.

Start with administrative or content workflows. Do not document dangerous machinery, medical care, legal processes, emergency response, financial controls, cybersecurity access, regulated operations, or other high-stakes work without qualified subject-matter review and formal approval.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one simple demonstration into a tested checklist or short SOP.
- **Medium:** Build reusable templates, decision trees, role-based approvals, version control, training checks, and scheduled reviews.
- **Hard:** Design an organization-wide process system with risk classification, controlled documents, audit trails, change management, access controls, performance metrics, automation, and regulatory oversight.

## What's current

Microsoft published 2026 guidance for drafting reusable SOP templates with Copilot in Word, including defining the audience, purpose, format, references, and human review. Google Docs can also create formatted drafts grounded in selected Workspace sources and incorporate reviewer comments. These tools reduce blank-page work, but neither replaces observation, owner approval, nor testing by the person who must follow the procedure.

## Free-first learning path

1. Choose a fictional or low-risk task and capture it with notes or a free screen recorder.
2. Use a free document editor and an AI tool with de-identified source text.
3. Draft the SOP with the prompt above and verify every step against the recording.
4. Ask another person to perform the task using only the document.
5. Improve the template before paying for specialized process-capture software.

## The takeaway

An AI-generated SOP is only a draft. A usable SOP is source-backed, owner-approved, versioned, and proven by someone who can complete the task without guessing.
