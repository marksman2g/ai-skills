# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's A1 AI lesson is:

**Create one simple team rule for using AI.**

Recent workplace AI research shows a useful pattern. Gallup reported on July 27, 2026 that AI can improve individual productivity, but many organizations are still not seeing major workflow transformation. The difference is often not the tool itself. It is whether people get clear guidance, manager support, and practical rules for using AI in real work.

That matters for small businesses too.

If everyone uses AI differently, results become inconsistent.

If the team has one clear rule, AI becomes easier to trust.

## The Simple Lesson

A good AI rule answers three questions:

1. When should we use AI?
2. What must we check?
3. When does a human need to approve the result?

That is enough to turn casual AI use into a repeatable business habit.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act like an AI policy coach for a small business.

We want one simple rule for using AI on this task:
[describe task]

Create a practical team rule that includes:
1. When AI is allowed
2. What AI can help with
3. What AI is not allowed to do
4. What the person must check
5. When a manager or owner must approve the result
6. Examples of good use
7. Examples of risky use

Keep the rule short enough to put on one page.
```

## Example

Task: customer email replies.

Team rule:

```text
AI may be used to summarize the customer's question and draft a reply.
AI may not invent prices, policies, dates, availability, refunds, guarantees, or legal language.
Before sending, the person must check facts, tone, customer name, promised next step, and any business policy.
Owner approval is required for refunds, disputes, contract terms, medical/legal/financial advice, or unhappy customers.
```

This rule is simple, but it prevents the biggest problems.

## Why This Is Sellable

Many businesses already have people experimenting with AI.

The missing piece is structure.

A simple AI rule can help a business:

- Reduce inconsistent answers
- Protect customer trust
- Avoid accidental promises
- Keep private information safer
- Train helpers faster
- Move from random prompts to repeatable workflows

That is a practical service a beginner can learn and sell.

## The Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Write one AI use rule
- Create a review checklist
- Build a reusable prompt
- Make examples for one task

Medium:

- Create a small AI playbook for multiple tasks
- Train a team on safe AI use
- Build templates for customer service, marketing, and operations
- Track which AI workflows save time

Hard:

- Create AI governance systems
- Build agent workflows with approval gates
- Connect AI to business tools
- Monitor risk, accuracy, cost, and adoption

The advanced work earns more because it helps a whole team. But the first step is one clear rule for one common task.

## Try This Today

Pick one task where AI could help:

- Customer replies
- Social posts
- Meeting summaries
- Research summaries
- Quote follow-ups
- Weekly reports
- Inbox triage

Then fill this out:

```text
Task:

AI is allowed to:

AI is not allowed to:

Person must check:

Owner approval required when:

Good example:

Risky example:
```

Keep it to one page.

That one page can become a service sample, a website resource, or part of a beginner AI training offer.

## A1 Takeaway

AI productivity does not come from handing everyone a tool and hoping for the best.

It comes from clear use cases, review rules, and repeatable habits.

Start with one rule. Make it useful. Then build the next one.
