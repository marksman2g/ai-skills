Subject: Turn approved project records into a trustworthy closeout pack with AI

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for documenting delivery, acceptance, handoff, open items, and lessons without inventing success or assigning blame.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Finishing the work is not the same as closing the project. A business still needs to confirm what was delivered, who accepted it, what remains open, who will support it, which records must be preserved, and what the team should learn next time.

AI can organize those records into a useful draft. The professional skill is creating a **source-backed closeout pack**, not letting AI declare victory, sign for the client, or turn one person's opinion into an official lesson.

## Today's lesson: Keep four finish lines separate

Track each deliverable through four distinct states:

- **DELIVERED:** The item was provided at a recorded date and location.
- **VERIFIED:** The agreed acceptance checks were performed and recorded.
- **ACCEPTED:** The authorized customer or sponsor approved the result.
- **TRANSITIONED:** Ownership, access, documentation, support, and training moved to operations or the client.

Use `NOT CONFIRMED` when the evidence does not establish a state. A “done” task, a sent email, or an AI summary is not automatically formal acceptance.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional project or records you are authorized to process. Keep confidential contracts, credentials, personal information, payment details, and regulated data out of unapproved AI tools.

1. List every approved deliverable, acceptance criterion, owner, and required approver.
2. Link each item to its final version, delivery evidence, test result, and acceptance record.
3. Mark the four states separately: delivered, verified, accepted, and transitioned.
4. Create an open-items list for defects, pending approvals, warranties, invoices, training, support, access removal, or follow-up work.
5. Build a handoff checklist for documents, systems, contacts, permissions, maintenance, escalation, backups, and review dates.
6. Compare planned and actual scope, dates, cost, and results only with approved records.
7. Collect lessons from multiple roles using facts, observations, and suggestions as separate fields.
8. Ask AI to group similar lessons without removing minority views or naming a person as the cause.
9. Assign each approved improvement an owner, destination, and review date.
10. Obtain formal closeout approval, archive the records according to policy, and record what remains outside the closed project.

```text
Create:

2. deliverable register with DELIVERED, VERIFIED, ACCEPTED, and TRANSITIONED
   shown as separate states

4. open items, owner, next action, due date, and destination after closeout
5. operations handoff checklist
6. final scope, schedule, cost, quality, and outcome comparison
7. decisions and approved changes
8. lessons learned: evidence, observation, suggested improvement, and owner
9. records, retention location, access, and review date
10. approvals required before the project can close

Rules:
- do not declare a deliverable accepted without an authorized acceptance record
- do not invent benefits, savings, outcomes, test results, costs, dates, or owners
- do not treat task completion as customer acceptance
- do not assign blame, diagnose motives, or rank people
- preserve conflicting feedback and minority observations
- separate verified facts from participant opinions and proposed improvements
- show NOT CONFIRMED when evidence is missing
- cite the source and version for every important claim
- do not close tasks, revoke access, release resources, send messages, or archive files

Approved project records:
[Insert authorized baseline, delivery, test, acceptance, handoff, and feedback records]
```

## Human review checklist

- The latest approved scope and deliverable list were used.
- Final versions and delivery locations are correct.
- Acceptance criteria were tested and evidence is linked.
- The recorded approver had authority to accept each deliverable.
- Delivered, verified, accepted, and transitioned are not treated as synonyms.
- Open defects, obligations, payments, warranties, risks, and follow-up work remain visible.
- Operations has the access, documentation, training, contacts, and support path it needs.
- Confidential information and permissions follow records and privacy policies.
- Performance comparisons use approved baseline and actual data.
- Lessons distinguish evidence, observations, opinions, and proposed improvements.
- No person is blamed or evaluated by AI.
- Improvement actions have owners, destinations, and review dates.
- The sponsor or authorized owner approved closure and record retention.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI-Assisted Project Closeout Pack** for one small, low-risk project. Deliver a source register, deliverable-and-acceptance table, open-items list, handoff checklist, lessons-learned draft, improvement-action list, archive index, and approval page.

The client supplies the authoritative records, acceptance authority, specialists, retention rules, and final approval. You organize evidence and prepare a reviewable draft; you do not certify delivery, accept work, evaluate employees, close contracts, revoke access, or declare benefits achieved.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one approved deliverable list and its evidence into a closeout checklist with visible gaps.
- **Medium:** Build reusable acceptance registers, handoff templates, open-item tracking, retrospective workflows, archive indexes, and improvement follow-up.
- **Hard:** Design governed enterprise closeout with contract and financial reconciliation, identity deprovisioning, records retention, benefits realization, operational readiness, audit controls, portfolio lessons, and monitored AI agents.

## What's current

PMI guidance describes project closing as obtaining formal acceptance, transitioning deliverables to operations, completing administrative and contractual work, preserving records, and capturing lessons for future projects. It makes clear that acceptance comes from the appropriate sponsor or customer, not from the project team merely submitting an item.

The Association for Project Management's 2025 closure walkthrough presents seven practical steps: gain agreement to close, assess what was achieved, finish commitments, capture lessons, release the team, complete handover, and recognize the work.

The useful AI boundary is straightforward: AI can organize records and discussion, while authorized people verify results, accept deliverables, decide what remains open, and approve closure.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional project in a free document or spreadsheet.
2. Build the four-state deliverable register before asking AI to summarize it.
3. Run a short 4Ls retrospective: loved, longed for, loathed, and learned.
4. Verify every lesson against evidence or label it as participant feedback.
5. Complete HP LIFE's free AI for Beginners certificate or PMI's free KICKOFF badge course.
6. Continue with IBM SkillsBuild's free Project Management Fundamentals credential.
7. Consider the paid APM Project Fundamentals Qualification only when formal project credentials support your goals.

## The takeaway

AI can make closeout faster to assemble, but closure still depends on evidence, authorized acceptance, a real handoff, visible open items, careful records, and lessons that improve the next project without rewriting the past.
