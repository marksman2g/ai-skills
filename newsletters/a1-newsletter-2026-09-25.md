# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's practical lesson is: **inventory the AI tools a business already uses before recommending another one.**

Recent small-business coverage highlights tight experimentation budgets, AI features already bundled into existing software, and tools that quietly become unused monthly subscriptions. A simple inventory makes the cost and business purpose visible.

## The 30-Minute Inventory

Create one row for every AI tool, AI feature, browser extension, automation, meeting assistant, or paid chatbot account:

```text
TOOL | OWNER | BUSINESS TASK | USERS | DATA USED | CONNECTED APPS |
MONTHLY COST | LAST USED | RESULT MEASURED | KEEP / TEST / REVIEW
```

Check business-card charges, app-store subscriptions, browser extensions, connected-app permissions, and AI features already included in email, CRM, design, accounting, or office software.

Do not cancel or disconnect anything during discovery. First identify the owner, dependency, data, and business impact.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act as an AI tool inventory assistant.

Using only the inventory below:
1. Group tools by business task.
2. Flag possible duplicate capabilities.
3. Flag tools with no named owner, recent use, measured result, or known cost.
4. List questions a business owner must answer before keeping, testing, or reviewing each tool.
5. Do not recommend cancellation or a replacement without more evidence.

Inventory:
[paste a version with no passwords, keys, private customer data, or secrets]
```

## Three Decisions

- **Keep:** actively used, owned, approved, and producing a measured result.
- **Test:** potentially useful, but needs a defined 30-day test and success measure.
- **Review:** unused, duplicated, unowned, unexpectedly connected, or handling unclear data.

Review does not automatically mean cancel. A tool may support a workflow you have not yet discovered.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer an **AI Tools and Subscription Inventory**.

Deliverables: current-tool list, owner and purpose map, duplicate-feature questions, monthly-cost summary, permission-review list, and a 30-day test plan for one tool.

Learning curve: easy to medium. Start with interviews and public billing information. Do not change accounts, permissions, subscriptions, or security settings without explicit owner approval and a recovery plan.

## Skill Ladder

- Easy: list tools, owners, tasks, use, and cost.
- Medium: compare overlapping features and measure workflow results.
- Hard: audit permissions, integrations, data flows, vendors, security, and compliance with qualified specialists.

## Takeaway

The cheapest useful AI tool may be one the business already pays for. Inventory first, test second, buy last.
