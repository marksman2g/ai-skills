# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Email overload is not just a reading problem. Important work gets buried because requests, deadlines, decisions, and unanswered questions are scattered across long threads.

AI can help surface those items. The useful skill is reviewing its suggestions before they become tasks, replies, or promises.

## Today's lesson: Triage before you draft

Sort messages into four queues:

- **Act:** A clear request requires your response or work.
- **Waiting:** Someone else owns the next step.
- **Reference:** Useful information with no action required.
- **Review:** The sender, request, deadline, sensitivity, or legitimacy is unclear.

Do not ask AI to "handle my inbox." Give it a narrow job: identify possible actions and show the evidence. You decide what is real, important, and safe.

## Try this in 20 minutes

Practice with 10 fictional messages or approved low-risk emails.

1. Define what **urgent** means for this inbox. A useful rule might be due within 24 hours, blocks another person, or affects a confirmed customer commitment.
2. Exclude spam, suspicious messages, personal data, confidential material, payment requests, and anything outside the approved AI environment.
3. Ask AI to classify each message as Act, Waiting, Reference, or Review.
4. Require the sender, requested action, owner, deadline, supporting sentence, and confidence for every proposed task.
5. Compare each result with the original message and thread.
6. Confirm dates, time zones, names, links, attachments, amounts, and recipients manually.
7. Draft replies only after the action list is correct.
8. Send nothing until a person reviews the full message and recipient fields.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious email-triage assistant. Use only the approved messages below.
Do not send messages, open links, download files, create commitments, infer urgency,
or invent missing facts.

Classify each message as Act, Waiting, Reference, or Review.

Return a table with:
message ID, sender, category, requested action, owner, exact deadline and time zone,
supporting sentence, confidence, sensitivity warning, and human check needed.

Use "Not stated" for missing details. Put suspicious requests, changes to payment
details, credential requests, unexpected attachments, conflicting instructions,
and unclear senders in Review. Separate explicit commitments from suggestions.

After the table, list:
1. Confirmed actions due soon
2. Questions that must be answered
3. Messages that appear informational only
4. Draft replies for human review, using no promises beyond the source

Approved urgency rule:
[Insert rule]

Approved low-risk messages:
[Paste messages with stable IDs]
```

## Before sending any AI-assisted reply

- Verify the sender and the full thread.
- Confirm the **To**, **Cc**, and **Bcc** fields.
- Check names, dates, time zones, amounts, links, and attachments.
- Remove claims or promises that were not approved.
- Make sure the tone fits the relationship and situation.
- Treat payment, password, account, legal, health, employment, and identity requests as high risk.
- Use a trusted channel to verify unusual or urgent instructions.
- Read the final message once without relying on the AI summary.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **Inbox Workflow Setup** for a small business. Help define triage categories, urgency rules, reusable prompts, reply templates, review checklists, labels, and a daily action-list format. Teach the owner or staff to run and verify the routine in their approved email system.

This is safer and more repeatable than selling unattended email automation. Do not request a client's password or copy their mailbox into your personal AI account. Work through client-approved access, tools, retention rules, and supervision.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Summarize low-risk threads and identify explicit requests, questions, and deadlines.
- **Medium:** Build labels, templates, escalation rules, quality checks, and approved task handoffs.
- **Hard:** Design governed email operations with delegated access, identity controls, phishing defenses, retention, audit logs, customer-system integrations, and monitored automation.

## Free-first learning path

1. Build the four queues with ordinary Gmail labels or Outlook folders.
2. Practice on fictional messages using a free AI tool without sensitive information.
3. Save a reusable triage prompt and a final-send checklist.
4. Measure time saved and errors caught for one week.
5. Consider paid inbox AI only when the workflow is already clear and the account is approved for it.

## The takeaway

AI can shorten the distance between a crowded inbox and a useful action list. It should not shorten the distance between an unverified message and a costly decision.
