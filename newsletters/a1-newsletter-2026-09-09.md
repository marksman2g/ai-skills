# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's practical AI lesson is:

**Choose the workflow before choosing the AI tool.**

Recent business guidance emphasizes measurable value, governance, and human review. That matters because an impressive demonstration can still be a poor first project if errors are expensive or the output is hard to check.

## The Five-Factor Scorecard

List three repetitive tasks. Give each task 0, 1, or 2 points for every factor.

```text
FREQUENCY
0 = monthly or less
1 = weekly
2 = daily

TIME COST
0 = under 10 minutes
1 = 10 to 30 minutes
2 = more than 30 minutes

CLEAR INPUTS
0 = information is scattered or sensitive
1 = some information is organized
2 = approved information is easy to provide

EASY TO REVIEW
0 = requires expert judgment
1 = a trained person can check it
2 = errors are obvious and quick to correct

LOW CONSEQUENCE
0 = errors affect money, safety, legal rights, or customers
1 = errors cause moderate rework
2 = errors stay internal and are inexpensive to fix
```

Add the points. A task scoring 8 to 10 is a strong first candidate. A task scoring 5 to 7 needs tighter boundaries. A task below 5 should remain manual for now.

## Example

Task: turn internal meeting notes into a draft action list.

```text
Frequency: 2
Time cost: 1
Clear inputs: 2
Easy to review: 2
Low consequence: 2
TOTAL: 9
```

This is a better starting point than automatically sending invoices, approving refunds, or publishing customer-facing claims.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act as a small-business AI workflow reviewer.

Here are three repetitive tasks:
[describe each task]

Score each task from 0 to 2 for:
1. Frequency
2. Time cost
3. Clear, approved inputs
4. Ease of human review
5. Low consequence if wrong

Explain every score. Identify missing information instead of guessing.
Recommend one small pilot that keeps a person responsible for the final result.
Define what success should be measured after five test runs.
```

## Run a Five-Test Pilot

Do not connect the task to live systems yet. Run five examples manually with AI assistance.

Track:

- Minutes before AI
- Minutes with AI
- Review minutes
- Number of corrections
- Any missing or invented facts
- Whether the final result was usable

Count review time as part of the cost. If the owner spends longer correcting the output than doing the task, the workflow is not ready.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer an **AI Workflow Opportunity Scorecard**.

Deliverables:

- Interview covering five repetitive tasks
- Score for value, readiness, review effort, and risk
- Recommendation for one pilot
- Reusable test prompt
- Five-run measurement sheet
- Clear do-not-automate list

Learning curve: easy

Effort: 60 to 90 minutes after practice

Best buyers: small offices, local services, creators, nonprofits, consultants, and retail businesses.

Why people pay: the scorecard can prevent wasted subscriptions and risky automation projects.

Do not guarantee savings or revenue. Measure the buyer's actual workflow first.

## Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Interview the person doing the work
- Score candidate tasks
- Write a test prompt
- Record time and corrections

Medium:

- Map handoffs and exceptions
- Estimate review and maintenance costs
- Compare tools against written requirements
- Create a pilot report

Hard:

- Connect approved systems and data
- Add monitoring, logs, permissions, and alerts
- Test edge cases and recovery steps
- Measure operational and financial results over time

## A1 Takeaway

The best first AI workflow is rarely the flashiest one.

Choose work that happens often, uses clear inputs, is easy to review, and is inexpensive when wrong. Prove value with five manual tests before paying for automation.
