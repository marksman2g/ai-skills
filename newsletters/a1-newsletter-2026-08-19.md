# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's A1 AI lesson is:

**Capture the customer need before you automate the workflow.**

Current small-business AI guidance keeps pointing to the same practical truth: AI workflows work best when the business understands the task before choosing the tool. SCORE's current "Working Smarter" series starts with customer capture before automation and measurement. GoDaddy, Fabren, NextTeammate, and other current workflow guides say the same thing in different words: start with the work, define the input, keep a human review point, and measure the result.

That means a beginner AI service should not start with:

```text
Let me automate this.
```

Start with:

```text
Let me help you capture what the customer actually needs.
```

## The Simple Lesson

Before AI drafts a reply, builds a quote, writes a follow-up, or routes a lead, it needs a clear customer requirement.

A customer requirement answers:

1. What does the customer want?
2. What problem are they trying to solve?
3. What information is missing?
4. What decision does the business need to make?
5. What is the safest next step?

If those answers are unclear, AI should not move forward as if everything is normal.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act like a customer requirements assistant for a small business.

Read this customer message:
[paste message]

Create a requirements summary with:
1. What the customer is asking for
2. The business problem or need behind the request
3. Important details mentioned
4. Missing information
5. Risky assumptions AI should not make
6. Suggested next question to ask the customer
7. Suggested internal next step for the business
8. Whether this is a normal case or needs owner review

Do not draft the final reply yet. Capture the requirement first.
```

## Example

Customer message:

```text
Hi, I need help with a setup for next weekend. Can you let me know what it costs and if you are available?
```

Requirements summary:

- Customer wants: setup help next weekend
- Need: service availability and pricing
- Details mentioned: next weekend
- Missing information:
  - exact date
  - location
  - type of setup
  - size or scope
  - deadline
- Risky assumptions:
  - price
  - availability
  - service type
  - travel or setup requirements
- Next question:
  - "What date, location, and setup type do you need?"
- Internal next step:
  - check calendar and approved pricing rules
- Case type:
  - normal, but price and availability need human confirmation

That is better than letting AI invent a confident reply.

## Why This Is Sellable

Small businesses lose time when customer needs are unclear.

An AI-assisted requirements workflow can help:

- Reduce back-and-forth
- Catch missing information
- Prepare better replies
- Improve quote accuracy
- Route leads correctly
- Avoid wrong promises
- Create cleaner intake records

This is useful because it sits before automation. It makes every later workflow safer.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer:

**AI Customer Requirements Capture**

What you provide:

- One customer intake prompt
- One missing-information checklist
- One normal-case versus owner-review rule
- One internal next-step format
- One reply draft prompt that only runs after requirements are clear
- Three example customer messages

Learning curve: easy

Effort: low

Best buyer: service businesses, coaches, local shops, contractors, creators, nonprofits, offices, and anyone who answers customer questions manually.

Why people pay: they want fewer unclear requests, faster follow-up, and fewer wrong assumptions.

## The Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Summarize customer messages
- Identify missing information
- Write follow-up questions
- Create intake templates

Medium:

- Build a repeatable lead intake workflow
- Add customer labels and priority rules
- Create quote-prep checklists
- Track missing-info patterns

Hard:

- Connect intake to CRM, calendar, forms, and task systems
- Route leads automatically
- Build AI agents with exception handling
- Measure lead response time, conversion, accuracy, and missed follow-ups

The hard work can earn more, but customer capture is the foundation.

## Try This Today

Pick one customer message or request.

Before drafting a reply, fill this out:

```text
Customer wants:

Underlying need:

Details provided:

Missing information:

Risky assumptions:

Next question:

Internal next step:

Normal case or owner review:
```

If the missing information is clear, your reply will be better.

If the requirement is unclear, automation should wait.

## A1 Takeaway

Good AI workflows do not start with automation.

They start with understanding the customer request.

Capture the need first. Then draft, route, automate, or measure.
