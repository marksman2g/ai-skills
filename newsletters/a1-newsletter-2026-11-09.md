# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

A customer case study can help a business explain its value. It can also become misleading very quickly when AI smooths a quote, guesses a number, or turns one customer's unusual result into a promise for everyone.

The useful skill is not simply writing faster. It is building a small evidence system that lets AI organize and draft while people verify every claim and approve the final story.

## Today's lesson: Let the evidence write the claims

Before drafting, create an evidence table with one row for every important statement:

- **Source:** Interview timestamp, email, report, analytics export, or other approved record.
- **Exact evidence:** The customer's words or the recorded number.
- **Draft use:** Verbatim quote, approved paraphrase, background fact, or metric.
- **Metric context:** Baseline, ending value, timeframe, method, and data owner.
- **Permission:** Named, anonymized, internal only, or not approved for publication.
- **Status:** Verified, needs evidence, needs disclosure, or approved.

AI may help sort this table and draft from it. It should never fill an empty evidence cell with something that merely sounds believable.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional interview or an interview the customer has explicitly authorized for this exercise. Do not upload confidential material to an unapproved AI account.

1. Write down the intended publication channels, attribution, visuals, and approval process.
2. Confirm permission to record, process, quote, and publish the customer's information.
3. Divide the transcript into potential facts, quotes, outcomes, and follow-up questions.
4. Give every item a source location, such as `[Transcript 12:40]`.
5. For each metric, record the baseline, ending value, period, calculation, owner, and supporting file.
6. Ask AI to draft only from the evidence table and insert `[NEEDS EVIDENCE]` wherever support is missing.
7. Compare every quotation with the recording or approved transcript.
8. Check whether an anonymous story could still identify the customer through its industry, location, title, company size, or unusual result.
9. Send the complete draft, headline, quotes, numbers, visuals, attribution, and disclosures to the customer and the responsible internal owner.
10. Publish only the approved version and retain the approval record with the evidence table.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious case-study drafting assistant. Use only the approved evidence
provided below. Do not invent, combine, improve, or complete quotes, metrics,
dates, identities, causal claims, customer opinions, or endorsements.

For every factual sentence:
- attach its source marker
- preserve verbatim quotes exactly
- label paraphrases as paraphrases
- include the baseline, timeframe, and method for every result
- write [NEEDS EVIDENCE] when support is missing
- write [NEEDS CUSTOMER APPROVAL] for every quote or identity detail
- write [NEEDS DISCLOSURE REVIEW] for incentives or material relationships

Do not present one customer's result as typical. Do not claim that the product
caused an outcome unless the evidence supports that conclusion. If anonymity is
required, flag combinations of details that could identify the customer.

Produce:
1. an evidence-gap list
2. a 600-900 word draft with source markers
3. a quote-verification table
4. a metric-verification table
5. a final approval checklist

Approved evidence:
[Insert the authorized evidence table]
```

## Human review checklist

- Written permission covers recording, AI-assisted processing, attribution or anonymity, publication channels, and final approval.
- The AI tool and account are approved for the information being processed.
- Every quotation matches the recording or an explicitly approved edit.
- Every metric has a baseline, timeframe, method, owner, and supporting record.
- Unsupported causal language and exaggerated adjectives have been removed.
- Exceptional results are not presented as what everyone should expect.
- Payments, free services, publicity, or other material relationships receive an appropriate disclosure review.
- Anonymous stories have been checked for re-identification through combined details.
- The customer has approved the final headline, text, quotes, numbers, visuals, and attribution in writing.
- Legal, privacy, advertising, contractual, and regulated-industry questions go to qualified reviewers.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **Evidence-Backed Case Study Starter Pack** for one approved customer interview. Deliver a 600-900 word case study, source table, quote-verification sheet, metric evidence ledger, consent and approval checklist, basic anonymization review, and one customer revision round.

The client supplies the authorized interview and source records. You organize the evidence, draft from it, flag gaps, and coordinate approval. Unsupported claims are omitted or returned as follow-up questions.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one approved interview into one source-backed case study with verified quotes and a human approval step.
- **Medium:** Build reusable interview guides, evidence tables, consent forms, approval workflows, quote libraries, and approved social or sales versions.
- **Hard:** Design a governed customer-proof system connected to CRM records, identity and consent controls, analytics, legal review, localization, version history, expiration dates, audit logs, and re-approval rules.

## What's current

The FTC distinguishes a testimonial used in advertising from a customer review hosted on a review platform. Its current guidance says testimonials must reflect genuine experiences, material relationships may require clear disclosure, and businesses need support for claims made through endorsements.

The FTC also warns that a specific customer result can imply that others should expect the same result. A small disclaimer such as "results may vary" does not automatically fix that problem. The advertiser needs adequate support or an appropriate clear disclosure of generally expected performance.

NIST's de-identification guidance explains that removing a name may not be enough. Combinations of indirect identifiers can allow a person or organization to be recognized, so anonymized case studies need a re-identification check.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional interview and a free spreadsheet evidence table.
2. Watch HubSpot Academy's free case-study lesson to learn basic story structure.
3. Read the FTC's testimonial guidance before working with real customer stories.
4. Build a quote-verification table and metric ledger before paying for automation tools.
5. Draft one case study manually, then test how AI can organize the same approved evidence.
6. Add templates and workflow automation only after the human approval process works reliably.

## The takeaway

AI can accelerate the draft. Trust comes from consent, traceable evidence, exact quotes, verified numbers, careful disclosure, and written approval.
