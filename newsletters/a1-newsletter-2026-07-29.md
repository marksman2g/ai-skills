# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's A1 AI lesson is:

**Measure the workflow, not the AI.**

Current small-business AI guidance is getting more practical. Recent examples from GoDaddy, Bluevine, the U.S. Chamber Foundation, and small-business automation guides all point to the same idea: AI is most useful when it improves repeated work that already exists.

The mistake beginners make is judging AI by whether the answer sounds smart.

Businesses should judge AI by whether the workflow improves.

## The Simple Lesson

Before using AI on a business task, write down what success means.

Use simple measurements:

- Time saved
- Errors caught
- Replies sent faster
- Fewer missed follow-ups
- Better consistency
- Less repeated typing
- Clearer next steps

If you cannot measure the task, you cannot prove the AI helped.

## Use This Prompt

```text
Act like an AI workflow measurement coach for a small business.

I want to test AI on this repeated task:
[describe task]

Create a simple measurement plan with:
1. What to measure before AI
2. What to measure after AI
3. A daily tracking table
4. A human review checklist
5. What would count as success
6. What would count as failure
7. What to improve after one week

Keep it simple enough to use without special software.
```

## Example

Task: lead follow-up emails.

Before AI:

- Average reply time: 24 hours
- Missed follow-ups: 4 per week
- Time spent drafting: 15 minutes per message
- Common issue: replies are inconsistent

AI-assisted workflow:

1. Summarize the lead request.
2. Draft a reply using approved business details.
3. List missing information.
4. Add a follow-up task.
5. Human checks facts, pricing, promises, and tone.

After one week:

- Average reply time: 6 hours
- Missed follow-ups: 1 per week
- Time spent drafting: 5 minutes per message
- Templates saved: 3

That is useful proof.

## Why This Is Sellable

Small businesses do not want abstract AI.

They want to know:

- Did this save time?
- Did this reduce mistakes?
- Did this help customers?
- Did this make follow-up easier?
- Can my team repeat it?

If you can answer those questions, you can sell a practical AI service.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer:

**AI Workflow Measurement Setup**

What you provide:

- One repeated task selected
- One simple baseline measurement
- One AI-assisted prompt or checklist
- One human review checklist
- One weekly tracking table
- One before-and-after summary

Learning curve: easy to medium

Effort: low

Best buyer: small business owners, coaches, creators, nonprofits, service providers, and office teams using AI casually but not measuring whether it helps.

Why people pay: they want proof before investing more time, money, or trust in AI.

## The Skill Ladder

Easy:

- Track time saved
- Compare before-and-after outputs
- Save reusable prompts
- Build review checklists

Medium:

- Audit multiple workflows
- Rank tasks by time, risk, and value
- Create a weekly AI report
- Standardize team templates

Hard:

- Build AI agents with approval gates
- Connect workflows to CRM, inbox, calendar, or invoices
- Track performance, errors, cost, and privacy risk
- Create an AI operating system for a small team

The hard work can earn more, but measurement is the skill that makes the higher-value offer believable.

## Try This Today

Pick one task you used AI for recently.

Fill this out:

```text
Task:

How long it took before AI:

How long it took with AI:

What AI helped with:

What I had to fix:

Mistakes caught:

```

If the workflow saves time and stays accurate, keep improving it.

If it creates more checking work than it saves, pick a simpler task.

## A1 Takeaway

The business value of AI is not the tool. It is the improved workflow.

Measure the task. Review the output. Save the proof.

That is how beginner AI skills become something people can trust and pay for.
