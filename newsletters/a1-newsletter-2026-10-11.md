Hello,

Customers increasingly ask AI tools where to buy, who provides a service, or how a local business works. Today’s lesson helps you check whether those answers are accurate without paying for a specialized platform.

## Today’s lesson: Create an AI visibility baseline

An AI visibility check is not a promise that a business will be recommended. It is a dated record of what different tools said when you tested a consistent set of customer questions.

Start with public facts only:

- Business name, website, phone number, address or service area.
- Current hours, services, pricing information, and availability that the owner publicly confirms.
- Customer questions the website should answer.
- Public sources cited by the AI answer.

AI answers can vary by tool, location, account, and date. Record the evidence instead of treating one answer as a permanent ranking.

## Try this in 30 minutes

1. Confirm the business’s public facts on its website and Google Business Profile.
2. Write five questions a real customer might ask, such as “Who repairs air conditioners near [city]?” or “Does [business] offer weekend appointments?”
3. Ask the same questions in two available AI search experiences.
4. Record the date, exact question, whether the business appeared, the description, and cited sources.
5. Mark incorrect, missing, or unsupported claims.
6. Choose one important customer question the website does not answer clearly.
7. Draft a short, factual answer and have the owner approve it before publication.

Do not create fake reviews, false locations, invented awards, misleading comparison pages, or claims the business cannot prove.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a careful website content assistant. Using only the verified public facts below,
draft a concise answer to one real customer question. Start with a direct answer, then
add useful details such as service area, conditions, next step, and the date the facts
were verified.

Do not invent prices, availability, credentials, reviews, rankings, guarantees, service
areas, or business claims. Mark missing facts as [OWNER TO CONFIRM]. Write for a human
reader first. End with a list of every source fact used so the owner can review it.

Customer question:
[Insert one question]

Verified public facts:
[Insert approved facts and source links]
```

## Human review checklist

- The business owner confirmed all facts before publication.
- Name, address or service area, phone, hours, and website are consistent.
- The answer helps a customer instead of repeating keywords.
- Claims about prices, availability, credentials, and results have evidence.
- AI responses are dated and labeled by tool and exact question.
- Sources cited by the answer were opened and checked.
- No visibility, traffic, lead, or ranking result is guaranteed.
- The baseline will be repeated later using the same questions.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Local AI Visibility Check**. Deliver a spreadsheet containing five customer questions, dated results from selected AI search tools, cited sources, factual errors, and one owner-approved content recommendation. Keep account access with the owner and do not sell fake reviews, hidden content, or guaranteed placement.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Check five questions, public facts, descriptions, and citations.
- **Medium:** Build a recurring question library and improve verified FAQ or service-page content.
- **Hard:** Combine technical SEO, structured data, local profiles, analytics, content testing, and reputation management into a measured search strategy.

## The takeaway

AI visibility begins with accurate public information and genuinely helpful answers. Measure what appears, fix what is wrong, and avoid anyone promising a guaranteed recommendation.
