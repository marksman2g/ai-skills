Hello,

An impressive AI demo does not always mean the tool is right for your business. Today’s lesson shows you how to compare AI vendors against the same needs, costs, and safeguards.

## Today’s lesson: Build an AI vendor scorecard

Before reviewing products, write down the business problem you need to solve. Then score every vendor using the same categories:

1. **Business fit:** Does it solve the actual workflow problem?
2. **Total cost:** Include setup, subscriptions, usage fees, training, and maintenance.
3. **Data handling:** What information is collected, stored, shared, or used to train models?
4. **Control:** Can you export your information and leave the service easily?
5. **Reliability:** What testing, uptime, support, and recovery options are provided?
6. **Human review:** Where will a person check the AI’s work before it affects a customer or decision?

AI can help extract answers from proposals and organize them. It should not make the final purchasing, legal, privacy, or security decision.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use two fictional or publicly available product descriptions for practice.

1. Write one sentence describing the problem you want solved.
2. List three must-have requirements and three nice-to-have features.
3. Paste each product description into an approved AI tool.
4. Ask the AI to fill in the same comparison table for both products.
5. Mark every unsupported answer as **Not provided**.
6. Review pricing, privacy, contract, and security details yourself or with a qualified professional.

For real proposals, remove confidential information unless your organization has approved the AI tool and its data practices.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a careful comparison assistant. Compare the two vendor proposals below using
only the information provided. Create a table with these rows: business fit, included
features, setup work, total stated cost, data collection, data retention, model training
use, export options, support, testing, human review, contract term, cancellation, and
unanswered questions.

For each answer, quote or closely paraphrase the supporting proposal text and identify
which proposal it came from. Write "Not provided" when evidence is missing. Do not
invent features, prices, security claims, or legal conclusions. End with five questions
I should ask both vendors before making a decision.

Business need:
[Describe the workflow and desired result]

Proposal A:
[Paste approved text]

Proposal B:
[Paste approved text]
```

## Human review checklist

- Both vendors were compared against the same requirements.
- Every important claim has evidence from the proposal or official documentation.
- One-time and ongoing costs are separated.
- Data retention, model training, deletion, and export terms are clear.
- Missing information is treated as a question, not an assumption.
- A responsible person approves the final decision.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI Vendor Comparison Worksheet** for local businesses. You can organize public product information, standardize questions, and prepare a decision table. Keep the service administrative: do not promise legal, cybersecurity, privacy, or procurement approval unless you are qualified to provide it.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn two public product pages into a comparison table.
- **Medium:** Build a reusable weighted scorecard around a client’s workflow and budget.
- **Hard:** Design a documented evaluation process with pilots, risk reviews, measurable success criteria, and an exit plan.

## The takeaway

Choose the business need first and the tool second. A consistent scorecard makes sales claims easier to verify and unanswered questions easier to see.
