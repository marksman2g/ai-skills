# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Today's practical lesson is: **document how a business sounds before asking AI to write for it.**

Recent guidance points to manual review and approval as a major part of AI-assisted marketing. A concise voice guide helps produce better first drafts, but AI still needs human judgment for context, accuracy, cultural nuance, and originality.

## The 30-Minute Exercise

Collect three short writing samples the owner approves: an email, webpage paragraph, and social post. Remove private customer information.

For each sample, mark:

- Three words describing the voice
- Typical sentence length
- Words or phrases the business uses
- Words, claims, and tones it avoids
- How it gives instructions or calls to action

Turn the patterns into a one-page card. Do not ask AI to invent a personality from a business name alone.

## Copy This Prompt

```text
Analyze the approved writing samples below and draft a one-page brand voice card.

Include:
1. Three to five voice traits
2. Sentence and paragraph style
3. Preferred words and phrases
4. Words, tones, and claims to avoid
5. How the voice changes for email, website help, and social posts
6. Five short "sounds like us" examples drawn from the samples
7. Five generic or off-brand patterns to reject

Use only the samples. Do not infer the owner's identity, beliefs, humor,
demographics, promises, credentials, or opinions.

Approved samples:
[paste de-identified samples]
```

## Test the Card

Ask AI for two drafts of the same low-risk message: one with the card and one without it. Have the owner compare them without seeing which is which.

Check:

- Which draft sounds closer to the business?
- Which sentences feel generic or exaggerated?
- Did either draft introduce an unsupported promise?
- Which edit should become a new rule on the card?

Keep version numbers and review the card when repeated editing problems appear.

## Beginner Service Idea

Offer a **Brand Voice Starter Card**.

Deliverables: sample review, one-page voice card, channel-specific rules, approved and rejected examples, reusable drafting prompt, and owner-review checklist.

Learning curve: easy to medium. This service documents an existing voice; it does not replace full brand strategy, legal review, cultural review, or an experienced editor.

## Skill Ladder

- Easy: identify recurring language patterns.
- Medium: create channel-specific rules and an approval workflow.
- Hard: maintain voice consistency across teams, regions, campaigns, automation, audio, and video with rights and consent controls.

## Takeaway

AI writes more consistently when the business gives it real examples and clear limits. The owner remains the final voice.
