Subject: Turn an extra client request into a safer change-impact brief with AI

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for documenting possible scope, schedule, cost, resource, quality, and risk effects without approving the change.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Projects rarely stay perfectly still. A client asks for one more report, a different integration, an earlier launch, or a new approval step. The request may be valuable, but even a small addition can affect people, dates, cost, quality, risk, and work already promised.

AI can help organize the evidence and questions. The professional skill is producing a **reviewable change-impact brief**, not letting an AI draft approve new work or manufacture estimates.

## Today's lesson: Analyze first, decide second

Keep four things separate:

- **Request:** What the requester actually asked for and why.
- **Baseline:** What the approved scope, schedule, budget, and responsibilities currently say.
- **Impact assessment:** Evidence and estimates supplied by qualified owners.
- **Decision:** Approve, reject, defer, request more information, or choose an alternative.

Use these status labels throughout the draft:

- **SOURCE FACT:** Directly supported by an approved document or message.
- **OWNER ESTIMATE:** Supplied by the person responsible for that area.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A condition being used temporarily and awaiting confirmation.
- **UNKNOWN:** Not enough evidence exists yet.
- **DECISION REQUIRED:** An authorized approver must decide.

AI may compare and format these inputs. It should not turn an assumption into a fact or an unknown into a confident number.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use a fictional request or information you are authorized to process. Keep client contracts, confidential pricing, personal information, credentials, and regulated data out of unapproved AI tools.

1. Record the request in the requester's own words, including the desired benefit and requested date.
2. Locate the approved scope, deliverables, exclusions, schedule, budget, acceptance criteria, and change process.
3. Cite the exact baseline section affected by the request.
4. Ask AI to identify possible impact areas and unanswered questions, not to calculate final impacts.
5. Send effort, cost, schedule, staffing, security, privacy, legal, quality, and operational questions to their qualified owners.
6. Record each owner's estimate with its method, assumptions, confidence, and date.
7. Compare at least three options: approve as requested, use a smaller alternative, or keep the current baseline.
8. List the risks of both approving and declining the request.
9. Prepare a one-page decision brief with no recommended approval unless an authorized owner supplied it.
10. After the decision, update the change log and only then update the project baseline, tasks, budget, or client communication.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a cautious change-impact analyst. Use only the approved project baseline,
the submitted request, and owner-provided estimates below. Do not approve work.

Create a DRAFT change-impact brief with:
1. request summary and requested benefit
2. affected baseline sections with exact source references
3. possible effects on scope, deliverables, schedule, cost, resources,
   quality, security, privacy, compliance, operations, and contracts
4. dependencies and affected stakeholders
5. owner estimates, methods, assumptions, confidence, and dates
6. risks of approving and risks of declining
7. options: requested change, smaller alternative, and no change
8. open questions and required reviewers
9. decision record: APPROVE, REJECT, DEFER, MORE INFORMATION, or ALTERNATIVE
10. documents that would need updating after approval

Label every statement SOURCE FACT, OWNER ESTIMATE, ASSUMPTION, UNKNOWN,
or DECISION REQUIRED.

Rules:
- do not invent costs, hours, dates, staffing, probabilities, or benefits
- do not imply that the request is approved, scheduled, funded, or assigned
- do not change the approved baseline
- do not create legal, security, privacy, procurement, or compliance conclusions
- preserve disagreements between owners instead of averaging them away
- show "UNKNOWN - OWNER INPUT REQUIRED" when evidence is missing
- cite the source for every baseline fact and owner estimate
- keep possible additions separate from approved work

Approved inputs:
[Insert the authorized request, baseline extracts, and owner estimates]
```

## Human review checklist

- The request is recorded accurately and has a named requester and date.
- The current approved baseline and change process were identified.
- Every affected commitment has an exact source reference.
- Estimates came from accountable owners and include assumptions and confidence.
- Scope, schedule, cost, resources, quality, risk, benefits, and dependencies were considered.
- Security, privacy, legal, procurement, accessibility, financial, and compliance effects went to qualified reviewers when relevant.
- Risks of approving and declining are both visible.
- Alternatives are compared consistently.
- The approver has authority under the project's change process.
- No work started and no baseline changed before approval.
- The decision, rationale, conditions, and required document updates are recorded.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI-Assisted Change Request Brief** for one small, low-risk request. Deliver a source-backed request summary, baseline comparison, owner-question list, impact table, option comparison, risk list, decision page, and change-log entry template.

The client supplies the approved baseline, estimates, specialists, and authorized approver. You organize the evidence and prepare a reviewable draft; you do not price work, approve changes, alter contracts, or direct the team to begin.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one request and an approved baseline into a sourced impact-question brief.
- **Medium:** Build reusable forms, approval thresholds, estimate templates, change logs, option scoring, version history, and post-approval task updates.
- **Hard:** Design governed portfolio change control with contract traceability, financial modeling, capacity planning, security and compliance gates, automated routing, audit trails, benefits tracking, and monitored AI agents.

## What's current

Microsoft's current project guidance says change control should define when a formal request is required, which authority can approve it, and what contractual effects must be considered. Its scope-management guidance says legitimate changes should be assessed across schedule, budget, resources, quality, risk, goals, and success criteria.

Microsoft's 2026 responsible-AI guidance for project Copilot describes generated task plans, risks, mitigations, and status reports as editable starting points. It specifically says generated narratives may not accurately represent the project's true context and should be thoroughly reviewed before distribution.

That leads to a practical rule: use AI to locate gaps and structure the brief, then use qualified owners for estimates and authorized people for decisions.

## Free-first learning path

1. Practice with a fictional project and a simple document or spreadsheet.
2. Build a baseline-versus-request table manually.
3. Ask AI only for impact questions until accountable owners provide estimates.
4. Compare the requested change with a smaller alternative and the no-change option.
5. Complete PMI's free KICKOFF course and earn its shareable badge.
6. Use Microsoft's free beginner generative-AI learning path to improve grounding and review skills.
7. Buy project or approval software only after the manual process is clear and repeatable.

## The takeaway

AI can make a change request easier to understand, but it cannot authorize extra work or know the true impact without owner evidence. A trustworthy brief keeps the baseline intact, exposes unknowns, compares options, and records a real decision before the project changes.
