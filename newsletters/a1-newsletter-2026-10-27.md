Subject: Turn a voice note into a useful business brief

Preview text: A 20-minute AI workflow for converting spoken ideas into a structured first draft without losing facts, questions, or the speaker's real intent.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Some people think more clearly out loud than at a blank page. A quick voice note can capture useful ideas, but the recording often mixes facts, guesses, side thoughts, and unfinished decisions.

AI can organize that raw material into a first draft. The valuable skill is preserving what the speaker actually said while making unknowns and next steps visible.

## Today's lesson: Speak in labeled blocks

Before recording, name the document and talk through six blocks:

- **Purpose:** What should this document accomplish?
- **Audience:** Who will read it, and what do they already know?
- **Facts:** What information is confirmed?
- **Ideas:** What are possible options rather than decisions?
- **Questions:** What still needs research or approval?
- **Next steps:** Who may need to do what, and by when?

You do not need polished sentences. The labels give AI enough structure to produce a cleaner draft without pretending every thought is final.

## Try this in 20 minutes

Use a fictional idea or an approved low-risk project.

1. Write the six labels on a note before speaking.
2. Record a two- to three-minute voice memo in a quiet place.
3. Avoid private, confidential, regulated, or client-owned information unless the recording and AI tools are approved for it.
4. Transcribe the recording and save the original transcript unchanged.
5. Ask AI to create a one-page brief while separating facts, assumptions, decisions, and questions.
6. Require a source phrase or timestamp for important claims.
7. Compare names, numbers, dates, promises, and quoted language with the transcript.
8. Resolve the open questions before publishing or sending the brief.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a careful document-structuring assistant. Use only the approved transcript.
Do not invent facts, decisions, owners, deadlines, evidence, or quotations.

Create a one-page business brief with:
- title
- purpose
- intended audience
- confirmed facts
- ideas or options
- decisions explicitly stated
- open questions
- proposed next steps

Label inferred or uncertain material "Needs confirmation." For every important fact,
decision, owner, deadline, and number, include the supporting timestamp or short source
phrase. Preserve the speaker's intended tone, but remove repetition and filler.

After the brief, provide a verification table with:
draft statement, source evidence, status, and human check needed.

Approved transcript:
[Paste transcript with timestamps]
```

## Human review checklist

- The recording was made safely and with any required notice or consent.
- The untouched transcript remains available for comparison.
- Facts are separated from ideas, assumptions, and opinions.
- AI did not turn a suggestion into an approved decision.
- Names, numbers, dates, prices, deadlines, and promises match the source.
- Unknown details remain clearly marked instead of being guessed.
- Quotes are exact, brief, and used only with permission.
- The document fits the intended audience and purpose.
- A person approved the final brief before it was shared.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Voice-to-Brief Documentation Pack**. A client supplies an approved voice memo using your six-block guide. You return the transcript, structured draft, verification table, open-question list, and one revision after the client checks the facts.

Good starter projects include internal project updates, event ideas, standard operating procedure outlines, content briefs, and meeting-preparation notes. Do not record people secretly or accept sensitive legal, medical, employment, financial, safety, or identity information without an approved process and qualified oversight.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Turn one short voice note into a reviewed outline or one-page brief.
- **Medium:** Create reusable interview guides, branded templates, approval stages, and repeatable revision workflows.
- **Hard:** Build governed knowledge-capture systems with secure transcription, speaker identification, source citations, permissions, retention, search, integrations, and quality measurement.

## Free-first learning path

1. Record fictional ideas with the phone's built-in voice recorder.
2. Practice ordinary voice typing in free Google Docs or another approved transcription tool.
3. Use the six-block speaking guide and the prompt above with non-sensitive text.
4. Compare every draft with the source transcript and track corrections.
5. Consider paid voice-first AI only after the manual workflow reliably saves time.

## The takeaway

Voice makes starting easier. Structure and verification make the result useful. Treat the AI document as a first draft built from evidence, not as a perfect record of what the speaker meant.
