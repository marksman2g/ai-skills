# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

When people search for a nearby business, they often need a quick answer: What is new? Is there an event? Is an offer still active? What should they do next?

Google Business Profile posts can place current updates, offers, and events on Search and Maps. AI can help draft them, but the business owner must verify every detail before publication. A polished post with the wrong date is still the wrong post.

## Today's lesson: Draft from a fact card

Before asking AI to write anything, make a one-page fact card:

- **Business:** Exact public business name and verified location.
- **Post type:** Update, offer, or event.
- **Audience need:** The question this post should answer.
- **Approved facts:** Current service, product, event, or announcement details.
- **Dates and times:** Start, end, time zone, and any cutoff date.
- **Offer terms:** Price, eligibility, limits, exclusions, and redemption instructions.
- **Action:** The one next step and its verified destination link.
- **Media rights:** Who owns or approved the photo or video.
- **Owner:** The person who verifies and publishes the final post.

The fact card is the source of truth. If a detail is absent, AI should flag it instead of guessing.

## Try this in 25 minutes

Use your own business information or a fictional example.

1. Choose one real, current update that would help a nearby customer make a decision.
2. Confirm the business name, address, hours, phone, and website against the live profile and official website.
3. Select the correct post type: update, offer, or event.
4. Complete the fact card, including dates, terms, link, and media permission.
5. Ask AI for two concise drafts using only the card.
6. Require a separate list of missing or risky details.
7. Check every factual statement against the source.
8. Test the call-to-action link on a phone.
9. Preview the image for legibility and accurate representation.
10. Have the owner approve the post, then record the publication date and review date.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Act as a careful local-business content assistant. Use only the approved fact
card below. Do not invent services, availability, prices, discounts, dates,
locations, qualifications, customer results, reviews, urgency, or terms.

Create two Google Business Profile post drafts for the selected type:
- Draft A: direct and practical
- Draft B: warm and conversational

For each draft include:
- a clear opening line
- the customer-relevant details
- one approved call to action
- a short visual brief based only on approved media
- a factual-claim checklist with the supporting fact-card field

Keep the wording professional and useful. Do not stuff keywords. Do not place
an unverified phone number in the post text. Mark every missing detail
"NEEDS OWNER CONFIRMATION." If the post is an offer or event, verify that all
required dates, times, terms, and limits are present before drafting.

Approved fact card:
[Paste the completed fact card]
```

## Human review checklist

- The business profile is claimed and the editor is authorized.
- Name, address, phone, hours, and website match current official information.
- The selected format matches the message: update, offer, or event.
- Dates, times, time zone, limits, exclusions, and terms are complete.
- The call-to-action link works and leads to the expected official page.
- The post does not make unsupported performance, health, financial, or superiority claims.
- Photos and videos are accurate, current, and approved for business use.
- The post follows Google content rules and any industry-specific requirements.
- The owner has approved the final wording and media.
- Someone is assigned to edit or remove the post when information changes.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer a **Local Profile Update Pack**. The client provides current facts, approved media, and access to review the live listing. You deliver a verified fact card, two post drafts, one visual brief, a publication checklist, and a simple monthly update calendar.

Begin with drafting and quality control. Do not claim guaranteed rankings, leads, sales, or placement in AI answers. The business owner should retain control of profile access and final publication.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Draft one source-backed update and verify every visible detail.
- **Medium:** Build monthly update calendars, reusable fact-card templates, link tracking, approval workflows, media libraries, and performance reviews for several locations.
- **Hard:** Manage governed local presence across profiles, websites, directories, structured data, reviews, analytics, permissions, APIs, and multi-location change control.

## What's current

Google's current help documentation says Business Profile posts can share updates, offers, and events on Search and Maps. Posts may include text, photos, videos, dates, offer details, and action buttons. Google reviews published posts, older posts may be archived, and inaccurate or policy-violating content may not be approved.

Google also states that profile information can come from business owners and other sources. That makes consistency important: a draft should be checked against the live profile and official website instead of assuming one old document is still correct.

## Free-first learning path

1. Claim or review the free Google Business Profile for a business you are authorized to manage.
2. Practice with a fictional fact card before handling a client's live information.
3. Use Google's free help pages to learn post types and policies.
4. Draft in a free document and track approvals in a spreadsheet.
5. Publish manually until the fact-checking and approval process is reliable.
6. Review live posts monthly and correct outdated information promptly.

## The takeaway

AI can make a local update faster to write. The sellable skill is keeping it current, complete, traceable, and easy for the business owner to approve.
