# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

You’re looking at a shop sign in an unfamiliar language. Is the shop closed on Sundays, or does it close early? A camera translation can give you a quick first pass. The useful skill is knowing which details to double-check.

## Try it in five minutes

1. Choose a public sign with two or three lines, such as opening hours or directions. Leave faces, IDs, and private paperwork out of the frame.
2. Open Google Lens, select **Translate**, and point your camera at the sign or use a photo. Choose the source and target languages manually if the app picks the wrong ones. [Google’s visual translation guide](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/google-lens/google-lens-features/)
3. Compare the overlay with the original. Check whether each line was picked up, then look closely at days, numbers, units, and words like “except” or “only.”
4. If a line is missing, take a straight-on photo in better light and translate that line alone. Read it again with the surrounding text so you don't mistake an exception for the main rule.

**Quick test:** On a sign showing `Lun–Vie 9–18` and `Sáb 9–13`, look for two separate schedules: Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.; Saturday, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. If the second line is missing, the translation is incomplete even if the first line reads well.

For allergies, medication, legal terms, or safety instructions, don't act on a camera translation alone. Confirm with an appropriate qualified person or an authoritative translation.

## Take it further

- **Easy:** Practice on three public signs in one sitting.
- **Medium:** In a few practice sessions, build a side-by-side review sheet for a shop's hours, directions, and other low-risk signs. A starter service is preparing drafts and gathering corrections from a fluent reviewer.
- **Hard:** Maintain approved multilingual material across locations. Budget weeks or longer to build glossaries, assign qualified reviewers, and track revisions.
