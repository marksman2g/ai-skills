Subject: Compare vendor proposals with AI without letting the tool choose

Preview text: A 30-minute lesson for building a cited evaluation matrix, finding gaps, and keeping the final decision with authorized people.

# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Vendor proposals are designed to persuade. One may lead with a low price, another with a polished demonstration, and another with impressive claims that are difficult to verify. AI can help organize the evidence, but it should not quietly change the rules, fill missing answers, or pick the winner.

Today's skill is an advanced follow-up to our October vendor-comparison worksheet: build an **AI-assisted proposal evaluation pack** around requirements and scoring rules approved before the comparison begins.

## Today's lesson: Freeze the rules, then trace the evidence

Create one row for every approved requirement. Give each row a stable ID and record:

- whether it is mandatory or scored
- its approved weight or priority
- each vendor's exact response
- the proposal page or section supporting that response
- any exception, assumption, or missing information
- the evaluator's verified score and rationale
- any legal, privacy, security, financial, technical, or contract review required

Use clear evidence labels: `VERIFIED RESPONSE`, `PARTIAL`, `NOT FOUND`, `EXCEPTION`, `NEEDS SPECIALIST REVIEW`, and `OWNER DECISION`.

`NOT FOUND` does not mean “no.” It means the proposal did not provide enough evidence for the reviewer to decide.

## Try this in 30 minutes

Use fictional proposals or documents you are authorized to process. Do not place confidential bids, supplier trade secrets, personal information, contract data, or security details into an unapproved AI tool.

1. Copy the approved requirements and evaluation method into a locked reference table.
2. Assign every requirement an ID, mandatory-or-scored status, and approved weight or priority.
3. List cost components separately: purchase or subscription, implementation, migration, training, integration, support, usage, renewal, and exit costs.
4. Give AI one vendor proposal at a time and ask it to extract responses with page or section citations.
5. Mark unsupported claims `NOT FOUND`; do not let AI infer a feature, certification, price, date, or commitment.
6. Record deviations and assumptions without deciding whether they are acceptable.
7. Route contract, legal, privacy, security, finance, accessibility, and technical claims to qualified reviewers.
8. Have evaluators verify the cited evidence before entering scores.
9. Compare scores only under the approved method and document the reason for every tradeoff.
10. Record evaluator names, conflicts of interest, versions, approvals, and the final decision separately from the AI draft.

## Human review checklist

- The requirements, weights, and scoring method were approved before comparison.
- Every vendor was evaluated under the same published or approved criteria.
- Each important claim links to the correct proposal page, section, and version.
- Missing evidence remains visible instead of becoming an AI assumption.
- Mandatory requirements are separate from weighted preferences.
- Price is not confused with total cost or overall value.
- Discounts, renewals, implementation, support, usage, and exit costs were checked.
- Contract, legal, privacy, security, financial, accessibility, and technical claims reached qualified reviewers.
- Clarification questions are neutral and follow the organization's communication rules.
- Evaluators disclosed conflicts and did not see information they were not authorized to access.
- Scores include human-written evidence and rationale, not only a calculated total.
- Tradeoffs and the final decision were documented and approved by authorized people.

## A beginner service you could offer

Offer an **AI-Assisted Proposal Evaluation Pack** for a small, low-risk purchase. Deliver a requirements matrix, cited response extraction, gap-and-clarification list, cost-component table, exception log, specialist-review queue, evaluator checklist, and decision-record template.

The client supplies the approved requirements, evaluation method, authorized documents, specialists, purchasing rules, and final decision-maker. You organize evidence and prepare reviewable drafts; you do not select a vendor, negotiate, purchase, certify compliance, or provide legal, financial, security, or procurement advice.

## Skill ladder

- **Easy:** Extract cited answers from two or three low-risk proposals using fixed requirements and visible `NOT FOUND` labels.
- **Medium:** Build reusable matrices with mandatory gates, weighted criteria, total-cost components, exceptions, clarifications, version control, reviewer routing, and approval records.
- **Hard:** Design a governed sourcing workflow with sealed-bid controls, role-based access, supplier risk, conflict management, contract lifecycle, ERP integration, audit trails, and monitored AI agents.

## What's current

Current U.S. Federal Acquisition Regulation guidance provides a useful fairness model even for businesses that are not federal agencies: evaluation factors should support meaningful comparison, the factors and their relative importance should be stated clearly, and competitive proposals should be assessed solely on the specified factors. The documented record should include strengths, deficiencies, significant weaknesses, risks, and the rationale for tradeoffs.

NIST's Generative AI Profile identifies confabulation, information integrity, data provenance, and human-AI configuration as risks that require documented controls. In practical terms, a fluent AI summary is not evidence. Reviewers still need the source, context, approved method, and accountable human judgment.

## The takeaway

AI can make proposal evidence easier to organize. A defensible vendor decision still depends on frozen criteria, equal treatment, source citations, visible gaps, specialist review, conflict controls, and an authorized human decision.
