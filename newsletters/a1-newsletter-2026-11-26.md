# A1 AI Skills Newsletter

Hello,

Reading a page twice can make it feel familiar. Can you explain it with the page closed? Today, use AI to draft a short quiz that helps you find out what you can recall and what you need to revisit.

## Try it in 15 minutes

1. Pick one page you own or are allowed to share: class notes, a hobby guide, or an approved onboarding checklist. Keep confidential records out of unapproved tools.
2. Ask for five questions and a separate answer key. In Gemini Notebook, you can also choose **Quizzes** in **Studio** and customize the difficulty. [Google's quiz setup guide](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16958963?hl=en)
3. Check the key against the page before using it. Delete questions that depend on missing information, and rewrite any question with two valid answers.
4. Close the notes, answer from memory, then check the key. Tomorrow, retry the questions you missed without rereading first.

**Quick example:** If a fictional opening checklist says to log the fridge temperature before stocking shelves, ask “What do you log before stocking?” Don't ask “What's the safe temperature?” unless the source actually gives one.

## Copy this prompt

```text
Use only the notes below. Write five short-answer questions: three
recall questions and two simple scenarios. Put the answers in a
separate section. For each answer, identify the supporting heading
or line from the notes. Skip anything the notes cannot answer.

Notes: [paste one approved page]
```

## From practice to paid work

- **Easy:** Make and verify one quiz in a single practice session.
- **Medium:** After several practice sessions, offer a small onboarding quiz pack based on a client's approved handbook. Deliver the quiz, checked answer key, and source references; have the process owner sign off.
- **Hard:** Over weeks or longer, build a maintained question bank with version tracking, reviewer roles, and pilot testing. Keep AI-generated practice scores separate from employment or certification decisions.
